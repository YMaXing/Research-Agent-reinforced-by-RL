Locally, the model uses V = V0 exp(−cφ) and m_DM = m0 exp(−c′φ) with c, c′ treated as constants (O(1) expected, interior bounds |c| ≳ √(2/3) ≈ 0.82 and c′ ≳ 1/√2 relaxed). This yields the Fading Dark Sector model, implemented by modifying the CLASS Boltzmann solver for coupled background evolution and perturbations, then sampling posteriors via Cobaya’s Metropolis-Hastings algorithm on Planck 2018 + ACT DR6 + NPIPE PR4 CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and Union3/Pantheon+/DES Y5 supernova datasets. The source includes tables on statistical significances across dataset combinations and figures on scalar evolution, w(z) contours, and posterior distributions for c, c′, Ω_k, and hilltop variants. Coverage assumes single-field trajectories, fixed SM parameters, and small Δφ; multi-field or curvature extensions are tested but not preferred; Newton-constant variation is noted without dedicated fits.</s>
<s slug="The Dark Dimension and the Swampland" type="golden_local">The Dark Dimension and the Swampland paper applies Swampland criteria, including the Distance/Duality conjecture and dS distance conjecture, to observational data on the cosmological constant to predict a unique asymptotic corner of the quantum gravity landscape. The main result is a single extra mesoscopic “Dark Dimension” of length \(l \sim \Lambda^{-1/4} \sim 10^{-6}\) m together with a tower of light states whose mass scale satisfies \(m \sim \Lambda^{1/4}\). The paper includes a table of contents summarizing its structure. Key Swampland ingredients are the Distance/Duality conjecture (tower mass \(m \sim e^{-\alpha\phi}\)), the Emergent String Conjecture restricting towers to either string excitations or Kaluza–Klein modes, and the species-scale cutoff \(\hat{M}\) that replaces the 4-d Planck scale once the tower appears. For a Kaluza–Klein tower the species scale is given exactly by \(\hat{M} = m^{n/(n+2)}M_{\rm pl}^{2/(n+2)}\), where \(n\) is the number of decompactifying dimensions. Bounds on the exponent \(\alpha\) are derived from one-loop Casimir energy and the Higuchi bound: \(\frac1d\le\alpha\le\frac12\). The paper shows that consistency with torsion-balance tests (Newton’s law verified to 30 µm, implying \(m\gtrsim6.6\) meV) and neutron-star cooling bounds forces \(n=1\) and places the tower scale at the observed dark-energy scale \(\Lambda^{1/4}\approx2.31\) meV, yielding the micron-scale Dark Dimension. Phenomenological consequences include a tower of sterile neutrinos with mass \(m_{\nu_s}\sim\lambda^{-1}\Lambda^{1/4}\), an active-neutrino mass \(m_\nu\sim\langle H\rangle^2\Lambda^{-1/12}M_{\rm pl}^{-2/3}\), and the species scale \(\hat{M}\sim\lambda^{-1/3}\Lambda^{1/12}M_{\rm pl}^{2/3}\sim10^9{-}10^{10}\) GeV. Assuming no large mass hierarchies between active and sterile neutrinos fixes the Higgs vev at \(\langle H\rangle\sim y^{-1}\lambda^{-2/3}\Lambda^{1/6}M_{\rm pl}^{1/3}\), where the Yukawa coupling \(y\) lies in the range \(10^{-2}{-}10^{-3}\). The same scale \(\hat{M}\) is identified with the onset of instability in the Standard-Model Higgs potential and with the GZK cutoff on ultra-high-energy cosmic rays. A concrete F-theory realization is outlined: the Standard Model resides on a contractible GUT 7-brane whose volume satisfies \(1/e^2=V_{\rm SM}\hat{M}^4\sim10^2\), localizing matter while the Dark Dimension remains bulk. The paper notes that hypercharge flux can push the GUT scale above \(\hat{M}\) and that strong threshold corrections modify coupling running. Casimir-energy estimates give an explicit length \(l\approx7.42\) µm for a 5-d circle compactification. Data points supplied for article use are \(\Lambda\sim10^{-122}M_{\rm pl}^4\), neutron-star bound \(l&lt;44\) µm for one extra dimension, and the explicit Casimir factor \(\lambda_{\rm Casimir}=5\cdot10^{-5}\). Gaps in coverage include the absence of a concrete stabilization mechanism for the radion, lack of quantitative neutrino-oscillation spectra, and open questions on realizing quasi-dS vacua in large-volume F-theory.</s>

<s slug="Galilean Equivalence for Galactic Dark Matter" type="exploitation">Galilean Equivalence for Galactic Dark Matter examines whether dark matter (DM) obeys the equivalence principle (EP) with baryons on galactic scales. A phenomenological fifth-force potential \(V_\phi(r) = -\frac{g^2}{4\pi r} e^{-m_\phi r}\) between DM particles \(\psi\) yields an inverse-square attraction whose strength relative to gravity is set by the charge-to-mass ratio \(\beta \equiv g m_{\rm Pl}/\sqrt{4\pi} m_\psi\). When \(m_\phi=0\), the force is long-range; values \(\beta\gtrsim1\) and \(m_\phi^{-1}\gtrsim1\) Mpc had previously been invoked to evacuate dwarf galaxies from voids. The central observable is the morphology of tidal streams from a DM-dominated satellite. Energy scales satisfy the hierarchy \(E_{\rm orb}\gg E_{\rm tid}\gg E_{\rm bin}\), so stars (baryonic test particles) and the satellite (DM test particle) normally follow similar orbits. An attractive DM force displaces the stellar center of mass outward relative to the DM, preferentially ejecting stars onto higher-energy trailing orbits and depleting the leading stream. The opposite occurs for repulsive forces. Simulations were initialized with GALACTICS phase-space distribution functions for composite Milky-Way bulge-disk-halo models and evolved with a modified GADGET-2 N-body code that incorporates the DM force. Both host and satellite used truncated Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) profiles; the satellite parameters were \(5\times10^8M_\odot\), mass-to-light ratio 40, pericenter 14 kpc, apocenter 59 kpc. Four runs scanned \(\beta=0.0\)–0.3. Surface-density maps and angular profiles show the leading-to-trailing star ratio (measured in segments \(-300^\circ\) to \(-200^\circ\) versus \(350^\circ\) to \(450^\circ\)) falling from 0.66 at \(\beta=0\) to 0.0042 at \(\beta=0.3\). The Sagittarius (Sgr) dwarf, with observed leading and trailing streams from 2MASS M-giant and SDSS data, already excludes \(\beta\gtrsim0.1\) because its streams are roughly symmetric. Robustness tests (varying satellite mass, mass-to-light ratio, orbital eccentricity, spin, stellar binding-energy fraction, and host halo/disk mass) keep the leading-to-trailing ratio above 0.5 for \(\beta=0\) and below 0.2 for \(\beta&gt;0.2\). The work includes surface-density and ratio plots across these model variations. Current limits already rule out the \(\beta\gtrsim1\) regime proposed for void clearing; future astrometry (Gaia, SIM) plus refined DM distribution modeling could reach the percent level. Gaps remain in distinguishing multiply-wrapped leading versus trailing stars solely from radial velocities and distances, and in exhaustive exploration of baryonic physics or alternate host potentials that might mimic the asymmetry.</s>
<s slug="Phantom matters" type="exploitation">**Phantom matters** (David Andriot, LAPTh) proposes that the observed phantom regime (w &lt; −1) in recent DESI + CMB + Union3 data is physical and arises naturally from quintessence scalars coupled to matter (and optionally radiation). The effective dark-energy density and equation-of-state are defined by absorbing the variation of A_m(φ) (or A_r) into ρ_DE and w_DE: ρ_DE ≡ ρ_φ + (A_m − 1)ρ̄_m + (A_r − 1)ρ̄_r, w_DE ≡ [w_φ ρ_φ + (1/3)(A_r − 1)ρ̄_r] / ρ_DE. This construction automatically satisfies the observed continuity equation while allowing w_DE &lt; −1 whenever A_m grows with time, even though the underlying w_φ remains ≥ −1. The same coupling modifies the scalar equation of motion, effectively steepening the potential that can be used; the paper states that a phantom regime therefore requires steeper V than a non-phantom one. Two string-inspired realisations are solved numerically (single canonical field, radiation domination imposed at N ≈ −20). The first uses V = V_0 e^{−√2 φ} together with A_m(φ) = 1 + (1/8)(1 − e^{−√(2/3) φ}); the resulting solution yields CPL parameters w_0 = −0.67, w_a = −0.92, phantom crossing at z_c = 0.64, and lies inside DESI + CMB + Union3 error bars for 0 ≤ z ≤ 4. The second employs the 6-d volume scaling V ∼ e^{−√(8/3) φ}, A_r ∼ e^{√(3/2) φ}, A_m ∼ e^{√(3/8) φ} (normalised at today) and produces a comparable phantom regime with z_c = 1.07. Poles appear in w_DE whenever ρ_DE = 0. The first pole occurs near recent times (z ≲ 4) when A_m − 1 compensates ρ_φ; the second appears at radiation–matter equality when Ω̄_m + Ω_r = 1. Their presence is extremely sensitive to λ and A_mφ; a factor-of-two change in the latter toggles poles on or off. When no pole forms, w_DE(z) is convex and reaches a finite minimum; when a pole forms it is locally concave. Current DESI DR2 reconstructions (Chebyshev vs. Gaussian-process) disagree on concavity around z ≈ 2.5–3, leaving the question observationally open. An Early-Dark-Energy-like bump in Ω_DE appears systematically at z ≈ 10^3 because A_m − 1 becomes positive while Ω̄_m still dominates; the feature is an automatic output of every solution examined. The paper remains agnostic to fifth-force, varying-constant and dark-matter-mass constraints, notes that exponential forms are chosen for illustration rather than derived from a complete string compactification, and does not quantify the EDE bump’s impact on the Hubble tension. An appendix presents one additional hilltop-plus-linear-coupling example whose agreement with data is limited to z ≲ 3. The source includes an 8-line introductory table.</s>
