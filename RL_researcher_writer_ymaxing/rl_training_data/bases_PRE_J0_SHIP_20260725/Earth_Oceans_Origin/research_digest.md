<s slug="Building wet planets through high-pressure" type="golden_local">**Main topic and key concepts:** The source reports experimental results demonstrating high-pressure reactions between dense fluid hydrogen and silicate melts (olivine, fayalite, silica + Fe) that reduce Si^{4+} and Fe^{2+}, forming alloys/hydrides and releasing oxygen to produce H_{2}O (up to tens of wt%). This endogenic water production at core-envelope boundary (CEB) conditions offers a mechanism for water-rich sub-Neptunes and super-Earths to form inside the snow line from initially dry, H_{2}-enveloped rocky cores, implying an evolutionary link between H-rich and H_{2}O-rich planets without requiring migration. Key concepts include silicate-undersaturated conditions, redox evolution from Fe-Si alloy formation to SiH_{4} pathways, convective mixing of H_{2}O, ingassing, and miscibility of H_{2}/H_{2}O/SiH_{4} fluids. **Concrete examples, tools, frameworks, APIs, or techniques:** Experiments used pulsed laser-heated diamond-anvil cell (LHDAC) at APS 13-IDD beamline with 1,064 nm NIR lasers (105 pulses at 10 kHz, 0.1 s total heating), coaxial monochromatic X-rays (0.4133 Å or 0.3344 Å), Dectris Pilatus 1M CdTe detector, DIOPTAS for integration, PeakPo for unit-cell fitting, and Raman spectroscopy (532 nm Coherent VERDI) for OH/Si-H vibrations. Starting materials were San Carlos olivine (Mg_{0}._{9}Fe_{0}._{1})_{2}SiO_{4} + 20 wt% Fe, fayalite, and silica + Fe. Thermal evolution modeling solved 1D interior structure/evolution equations on a single mass grid (adopted from ref. 28 parameters) with EOS from refs. 57–59, incorporating convection, semi-convection, and grey-atmosphere irradiation. Supplementary Code 1–3 performed reaction stoichiometry and water-yield calculations. Artefact A01 is a 33-line table of experimental runs (P-T conditions, phases observed via XRD/SEM/Raman). **Specific data points, benchmarks, or claims:** At 6–22 GPa and ~2,900–3,017 K, reactions produced B2 Fe_{1-}ᵧSiᵧ (y=0.27), fcc/dhcp FeHₓ (x=0.22–0.4), SiH_{4}, MgO, and H_{2}O (Raman OH confirmed); 32% of Si^{4+} reduced in olivine + Fe runs, yielding 18.1(5) wt% H_{2}O from 4.5–5.7 wt% initial H_{2}. At 42 GPa, bridgmanite dissociated on melting to form alloys. For 5M_E planet + 5 wt% H_{2} envelope, CEB reaches relevant P-T for billions of years; X(H_{2}O) = 0.38–0.56 vs. prior models (10^{-4}–0.2). H:Si mass ratio ~0.06–0.08 matches sub-Neptune conditions; Mg:Si reduction from 2 to 0.5 increases water yield from 16 wt% to 29 wt%. Water mixing efficiency drops below ~3,500 K CEB temperature. **Notable limitations or gaps in coverage:** Heating times limited to 0.1 s per event; pressure uncertainty assigned at 10%; modeling assumes separate H_{2}/H_{2}O fluids despite full miscibility; equilibrium constants and critical X</s>
<s slug="Earth shaped by primordial H2 atmospheres" type="golden_local">Earth shaped by primordial H2 atmospheres explains Earth's water, ~10% core density deficit relative to pure Fe-Ni, and bulk silicate oxidation state (ΔIW ≈ −2.1) via equilibrium between H2-rich primary atmospheres and underlying magma oceans in progenitor planetary embryos of ~0.3–0.5 M⊕. The model places Earth formation in the context of exoplanet radius-valley populations, where rocky super-Earths formed with and then lost H2 envelopes over 10^8–10^9 yr by photo-evaporation or core-powered mass loss. The self-consistent thermodynamic model comprises 25 phase components (enstatite, MgO, SiO2, ferrosilite, FeO, Na2SiO3, Na2O, H2, H2O, CO, CO2 in silicate melt; Fe, Si, O, H in metal; H2, CO, CO2, CH4, O2, H2O, Fe, Mg, Na, SiO in atmosphere) linked by 18 linearly independent reactions. Equilibrium conditions plus mass-balance and surface-pressure constraints (Eq. 26) are solved by simulated annealing followed by ensemble MCMC sampling. Non-ideal mixing uses the ref. 33 activity-coefficient expressions for Si and O in Fe alloy (Eqs. 20–21); H in metal is treated as ideal. Silicate and atmosphere phases assume ideal mixing (sensitivity tests with regular-solution W parameters for H2O and H2 are reported). Equations of state are Vinet for molten Fe and third-order Birch–Murnaghan for silicate melt; core–mantle boundary pressures are ~40–60 GPa for 0.3–0.5 M⊕ embryos. Initial composition matches E-chondrite-like material (34.4% Fe metal, 0.07 wt% FeO, ΔIW ≈ −5.8). Fiducial run (0.5 M⊕, Tcore-mantle = 3000 K, initial H2 mass fraction 0.2%, Psurface ≈ 0.014 GPa after equilibration) yields metal containing 94.9% Fe, 3.8% Si, 0.8% O, 0.5% H (8% density deficit at embryo conditions, compressible to ~10% at 136 GPa), one terrestrial-ocean mass of H2O, and final ΔIW ≈ −2.1. Three-quarters of the deficit is attributable to H. Higher-T, lower-H trajectories require >6 wt% Si in metal and produce no H2O. Mars and Mercury lie below the ~0.2 M⊕ threshold for retaining H2 atmospheres above the silicate solidus (~1500 K) and therefore retain E-chondrite-like oxidation states and lack solar noble-gas signatures. The source includes Extended Data figures on embryo mass–temperature boundaries for magma-ocean + H2-atmosphere coexistence, Si wt% contours, pressure–radius profiles, and Fe and D/H isotope predictions. Sensitivity tests confirm robustness to non-ideal mixing and H-partitioning constants. Limitations include omission of S, C and N as core light elements, assumption of single-stage embryo equilibration, and simplified treatment of stratospheric D/H kinetics.</s>
<s slug="A nearly terrestrial DH" type="exploitation">Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko reanalysis shows dust grains in the coma preferentially adsorb HDO, releasing water with D/H up to 3–10× terrestrial (VSMOW) near the nucleus; at distances >120 km where gas is well mixed, the nucleus bulk value is (2.59 ± 0.36) × 10^{-4} (1.2–1.6× VSMOW). This aligns 67P with other Jupiter-family comets and reopens JFC delivery of Earth’s oceans. The protosolar nebula (PSN) D/H gradient, formed by decreasing isotopic exchange efficiency with heliocentric distance, is traced by water-ice ratios in comets, TNOs, and chondrites. Key concepts include the dust cycle (perihelion southern erosion, ~20% redeposition on northern hemisphere, reactivation pre-perihelion) and extended sources from sublimating ice-coated grains. Hypervolatiles (CO, CH4, HCN) correlate inversely with enriched D/H and ^{16}O/^{17}O during phase 3 (pre-perihelion) and positively during phase 1. Laboratory adsorption experiments and Hale-Bopp jet data support preferential HDO retention on dust surfaces. ROSINA DFMS supplied 4339 simultaneous D/H and ^{16}O/^{17}O plus 9177 ^{16}O/^{18}O measurements (full mission, 3.6–1.2 AU). Signals were extracted via double-Gaussian fits (Eq. 1) inside a Bayesian nested-sampling framework (dynesty) that marginalizes covariances between overlapping HDO and H_{2}^{17}O peaks at mass 19; H_{3}O was excluded after degeneracy, row-consistency, and correlation tests. Pixel-gain (5%) and gain-step (6%) corrections were propagated; observations within 2 h of instrument turn-on, >10° off-nadir, or poor fit quality (>50% uncertainty) were rejected. Post-perihelion data beyond 120 km yield stable ratios with minimal latitude or distance trends. Derived oxygen ratios are ^{16}O/^{17}O = 2214 ± 340 and ^{16}O/^{18}O = 392 ± 73, giving H_{2}^{18}O/H_{2}^{17}O = 5.65 ± 1.36 (matches MIRO). The paper includes Fig. 1 (solar-system D/H, ^{16}O/^{17}O, ^{16}O/^{18}O compilation), Fig. 2 (five-stage dust-enrichment model), Fig. 3 (latitude/distance trends across orbital phases), and Fig. 4 (phase-binned D/H averages). Limitations include residual seasonal effects, grain-size heterogeneity (µm to cm), incomplete modeling of turbulent PSN mixing, and reliance on post-perihelion geometry for the bulk-nucleus value.</s>
<s slug="Earth’s water may have been inherited from material" type="exploitation">Earth's water may have been inherited from material similar to enstatite chondrite meteorites. The main topic is the origin of terrestrial water, with the hypothesis that enstatite chondrite (EC) meteorites supplied sufficient indigenous hydrogen to account for Earth's oceans and mantle without requiring dominant late delivery from outer-Solar-System carbonaceous chondrites (CCs). Key concepts include hydrogen abundance reported as water-equivalent wt %, deuterium isotopic composition expressed as δD relative to SMOW, nitrogen isotopes (δ15N), petrologic types (EH3–EH6, EL6), thermal metamorphism effects on parent bodies, and mixing models of chondritic precursors for Earth’s accretion. Measurements used elemental analyzer–isotope ratio mass spectrometry (EA-IRMS) on bulk samples and secondary ion mass spectrometry (SIMS) on chondrule mesostasis. The study analyzed 13 EC meteorites spanning EH3 to EH6 and EL6, plus the aubrite Norton County. Bulk EC hydrogen contents range 0.08–0.54 wt % H2O (EH3 average 0.44 ± 0.04 wt %; more metamorphosed groups 0.2–0.3 wt %), all above the EA-IRMS detection limit of ~0.05 wt %. Least-metamorphosed EH3 samples average δD = –103 ± 3 ‰; metamorphosed ECs and the aubrite average –127 ± 15 ‰. SIMS on Sahara 97096 (EH3.1–3.4) mesostasis yields 2700–12300 wt ppm H2O (average 7560 ± 1546 wt ppm) with homogeneous δD = –147 ± 16 ‰. Comparison samples include Orgueil, Alais, Murchison (CI/CM, 7.2–9.1 wt % H2O), Vigarano and Kaba (CV), and ordinary chondrite pyroxenes. Fig. 1 shows bulk-rock and mesostasis H contents and δD values. Fig. 2 quantifies EC contributions across three mixing models (100 % EC; 68 % EC + 32 % CC; 71 % EC + 24 % OC + 5 % CC), reporting 3.4–23.1 ocean masses (1 ocean = 1.4 × 10^21 kg H2O) from bulk ECs and 3–4.5 ocean masses from identified phases alone. Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 compare δD and δ15N of ECs, OCs, and CCs against mantle ranges (δD –220 to –20 ‰; primitive mantle –130 to –75 ‰) and surface reservoirs. Tables S2 and S4 list the underlying data. EC-like material supplies at least three times the ocean mass even when only mesostasis (16.2 vol % of chondrules) and insoluble organic matter (0.6 wt %) carriers are counted; low-calcium pyroxene may account for an additional 15–58 %. Only EC bulk compositions simultaneously satisfy both mantle δD and δ15N; CCs match δD but not δ15N. A ~4 % CI-like contribution to the surface reservoir is calculated from H isotopes and ~15 % from N isotopes. Limitations include upper-limit estimates because volatile loss during accretion is unquantified, unidentified H carriers (possibly sulfur-rich carbon-bearing porous amorphous silica) for 65–80 % of bulk H, and the assumption that Sahara 97096 represents the most pristine EC composition.</s>
<s slug="Hydrogen Isotopic Composition of Hydrous Minerals in Asteroid Ryugu" type="exploitation">The source examines the hydrogen isotopic composition of hydrous minerals in Cb-type asteroid Ryugu samples returned by JAXA Hayabusa2, using in situ secondary ion mass spectrometry (SIMS) on fragments from stones A0040, A0094 (site A) and C0002 (site C). It compares results to CI chondrites Orgueil and Alais and CM chondrite Mighei, linking Ryugu to CI-like material and evaluating CI chondrite contributions to Earth's surficial reservoirs. Key concepts include aqueous alteration on the parent body forming serpentine and saponite, D/H ratios expressed as δD relative to SMOW (D/H_SMOW = 155.76 × 10^{-6}), and distinction between hydrous mineral hydrogen versus insoluble organic matter (IOM) or soluble organics. The zero intercept of the D^-/H^- versus C^-/H^- correlation isolates the hydrous mineral signal after least-squares fitting and instrumental mass fractionation correction via a calibration line (slope 2.2 ± 0.2, intercept [-1.16 ± 0.36] × 10^{-4}). SIMS was performed on the CAMECA IMS-1280HR2 at CRPG with a 10 kV Cs^+ primary beam rastered over 12 × 12 μm² areas, collecting H^-, D^-, ^{13}C^-, and ^{29}Si^- ions (mass resolving power M/ΔM = 3300). Reference materials included Kipawa amphibole, montmorillonite, serpentine, hydrogen-bearing glasses, terrestrial kerogen, and D-rich IOMs. Post-analysis BSE/EDS imaging with a polarized reflected-light microscope rejected analyses containing holes or cracks. Data points include 95 valid Ryugu analyses yielding D/H_Ryugu = [165 ± 19] × 10^{-6} (δD_Ryugu = +59 ± 121‰, 2σ), Orgueil hydrous minerals δD_Orgueil = -63 ± 138‰, and Mighei δD_Mighei = -388 ± 134‰. Ryugu and Orgueil values match prior CI estimates (+98 ± 99‰) and are higher than CM averages (-350 to -444‰). Bulk Ryugu shows δD = +252 ± 13‰ and 6.84 wt.% H_2O equivalent. Mass-balance calculations indicate ~3% CI-like contribution to Earth's surficial hydrogen, accounting for ~30% of ocean water mass. The source concludes Ryugu/CI water derives from interstellar ice partially re-equilibrated with nebular H_2. The source includes a 10-line table on sample Pearson coefficients and water data, plus a 12-line table on times and references for standards. It covers D-rich outliers (five in Ryugu) potentially linked to submicron organic hot spots observed by NanoSIMS ion imaging. Notable limitations include ±121‰ uncertainties on Ryugu δD, reliance on 90 of 95 points after outlier removal at 95% prediction interval, and assumptions that equilibrium fractionation between silicates and water is negligible relative to measurement reproducibility. The method cannot isolate NH_3 contributions or fully resolve soluble versus insoluble organics without additional high-resolution imaging.</s>
<s slug="Noble Gases and Stable Isotopes Track the Origin and Early" type="exploitation">Noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe) and stable isotopes (H, C, N, O, S) serve as tracers of volatile origins, accretion, outgassing, differentiation, and atmospheric escape on Venus. It includes a table (ARTEFACT_A04) on radiogenic isotopes, mixing ratios, and recommended precisions. Key concepts include primordial (non-radiogenic) isotopes fingerprinting sources and mass-fractionating loss, radiogenic isotopes (e.g., 40Ar from 40K, 129Xe from 129I) tracking I/Xe or K/U fractionation during early outgassing, and mass-dependent fractionation from hydrodynamic escape or ion pickup. <s slug="The Winchcombe meteorite, a unique and pristine" type="exploitation">The Winchcombe meteorite (CM2.0–2.4) is the most accurately recorded carbonaceous chondrite fall, with a pre-atmospheric orbit, short cosmic-ray exposure age (~0.3 Ma ²¹Ne, 0.27 ± 0.08 Ma ²⁶Al), and recovery within ~12 hours confirming minimal terrestrial modification. Its orbit (T_J = 3.121 ± 0.006, perihelion ~0.99 AU) links it to the 3:1 Jupiter resonance in the main belt, matching Sutter’s Mill and Maribo but distinct from Tagish Lake and Flensburg. Fireball data from 16 UKFAll cameras (UKMON CMOS/CCD, UKFN DSLR, SCAMP/FRIPON all-sky, NEMETODE, GMN, AllSky7) plus eyewitness reports gave an entry velocity of 13.5 ± 0.01 km s⁻¹ and mass of 13 ± 3 kg; micro-XCT and cosmogenic nuclides constrain pre-atmospheric diameter to 0.3 ± 0.1 m. Petrography (ZEISS EVO 15LS SEM/EDS, JEOL ARM200F STEM, JOEL 2100 TEM, PANalytical/Enraf-Nonius XRD) shows >80 vol% Mg-rich serpentine-group phyllosilicates, tochilinite-cronstedtite intergrowths, calcite/dolomite, magnetite framboids, and rare anhydrous silicates/metal, recording low-temperature (<150 °C) aqueous alteration on the parent body. Bulk density ~2090 kg m⁻³; macroporosity 0.6–4.2%. Noble-gas data indicate near-surface regolith origin with fractionated solar-wind components. Hydrogen stepwise pyrolysis (SUERC) yielded 10.5 ± 1.1 wt% water and δD = −142 ± 4‰ (SMOW), close to the terrestrial hydrosphere range (−400 to +50‰) after exclusion of <200 °C adsorbed water. Stepped combustion (Open University Finesse) gave 2.0 ± 0.1 wt% C (δ¹³C = −1.7 ± 1.1‰) and 433.8 ± 20.3 ppm N (δ¹⁵N = 16.7 ± 0.9‰). TGA (TA SDT Q600) confirmed low weathering (0.6 wt% loss 100–200 °C). LC-MS and GC-MS (Agilent 7890A/5975C, CP-Chirasil columns) detected α-aminoisobutyric acid (467 ± 17 ng g⁻¹), isovaline (391 ± 17 ng g⁻¹), racemic protein amino acids, lipids, fatty acids, and nanoglobules (low-voltage FEI Quanta SEM-EDS, Bruker FlatQUAD). Reflectance spectra (VOSEG BRDF, Bruker VERTEX 70v) match hydrated C-type asteroids (albedo 4.09 ± 0.18%). These data support the hypothesis that volatile-rich CM-like asteroids delivered Earth’s water and prebiotic organics. Limitations include brecciation recording possible parent-body disruption, restriction to CM lithologies, and absence of direct Ryugu/Bennu sample comparisons.</s>
<s slug="The source of hydrogen in earth’s building blocks" type="exploitation">The source examines the origin and abundance of hydrogen in enstatite chondrites (ECs) as potential building blocks of the proto-Earth, focusing on whether this hydrogen is native and sufficient to account for Earth's present-day water budget. The central finding is that hydrogen bonded to sulfur (H-S) occurs throughout the EH3 chondrite LAR 12252, with concentrations highest in fine matrix and linked to submicron pyrrhotite (Fe_{1-x}S, 0 < x < 0.125). S-XANES spectroscopy at the sulfur K-edge was performed at Diamond Light Source beamline I18 using a 5 μm spot size stepped across a 470 × 480 μm² clastic matrix region (9024 spectra total). Spectra were collected from 2465–2495 eV (0.2 eV steps, 0.05 s dwell) and fitted with multiple Gaussian curves in IGOR Pro over 2465–2478 eV to extract amplitudes of the Fe-S peak (~2471.2–2478 eV) and H-S peak (~2473.2 eV); a separate Gaussian isolated the S^{6+} peak (2483.4 eV). Spot spectra were also acquired in chondrule mesostasis. Peak energies and shapes were compared against reference spectra (Anzures et al., 2019; Klimm et al., 2012; Lerner et al., 2021) and the ESRF Database of Inorganic Sulfur Compounds. Fine matrix contains an average of 9.8 ± 3.1 times the H-S amplitude measured in chondrule mesostasis (2 SE). Adopting matrix parameters from paired EH3 chondrites SAH 97072/97096 (21.5 vol% clastic matrix, ~50 vol% fine matrix, grain density 3730 kg m^{-3}, bulk density 3240 kg m^{-3}), fine matrix comprises ~4.8 wt% of the meteorite and contributes ~5 times more H-S than mesostasis. Combined with prior mesostasis H measurements, this indicates fine matrix supplies several tens of wt% of the bulk hydrogen budget. The H-S peak amplitude scales directly with the abundance of pyrrhotite rather than troilite, consistent with submicron pyrrhotite rims and grains observed petrographically. The proposed formation pathway involves low-temperature sulfidation of troilite to pyrrhotite in a high-fS_{2} nebular environment, followed by sacrificial catalysis with nebular H_{2} during flash-heating events to generate H_{2}S that dissolves in adjacent molten silicate and is trapped upon rapid quenching to glass. This assemblage matches the observed spatial association of H-S with submicron sulfide grains encased in SiO_{2}-rich glass. Bulk EC hydrogen concentrations (0.08–0.54 wt% H_{2}O) reported by Piani et al. (2020) and Thomassin et al. (2023) can therefore be explained by native H-S. The 5 μm beam size precludes direct confirmation that pyrrhotite itself hosts the hydrogen; absolute H concentrations remain difficult to extract because of sample heterogeneity and the absence of a complete meteoritic reference database. The proportion of fine matrix was taken from paired meteorites rather than LAR 12252 itself.</s>
<s slug="rosetta-fuels-debate-on-origin-of-earth-s-oceans" type="exploitation">Rosetta’s measurements of water vapour from Comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko show a deuterium-to-hydrogen (D/H) ratio more than three times higher than Earth’s oceans, challenging models that assign Jupiter-family comets a primary role in delivering terrestrial water. The main topic is the origin of Earth’s oceans under the hypothesis that the planet formed too hot 4.6 billion years ago for primordial water to remain, requiring later delivery by comets and asteroids whose relative contributions remain debated. The D/H ratio serves as the key tracer because theoretical simulations predict it varies with heliocentric distance and time in the first few million years of Solar System evolution. ROSINA (Rosetta Orbiter Spectrometer for Ion and Neutral Analysis) performed the in-situ measurements using its double focusing mass spectrometer (DFMS), reflectron time of flight mass spectrometer (RTOF), and cometary pressure sensor (COPS). The reported D/H value derives from HD16O/H2 16O ratios extracted from more than 50 DFMS spectra collected between 8 August and 5 September 2014, shortly after arrival on 6 August. Prior benchmarks include the 2011 Herschel detection that Comet 103P/Hartley 2 matches Earth’s D/H ratio, while 10 other comets (both Jupiter-family and Oort-cloud) exhibit a wide range of higher values. Asteroidal meteorites from the Asteroid Belt also match Earth’s ratio despite lower bulk water content. Comet 67P is classified as a Jupiter-family comet whose orbit is now controlled by Jupiter, with perihelion between Earth and Mars and aphelion beyond Jupiter (period ~6.5 years). Dynamical models place its formation in the Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune, contrasting with long-period Oort-cloud comets that originated in the Uranus–Neptune region before scattering. The ROSINA result exceeds even the highest previously measured Oort-cloud values, prompting the suggestion that Jupiter-family comets formed across a wider radial range than previously assumed. The summary draws on the peer-reviewed paper “67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko, a Jupiter Family Comet with a high D/H ratio” (Altwegg et al., Science, 10 December 2014). Coverage is limited to the initial post-arrival phase; later orbital evolution, seasonal changes, and Philae surface data are not included. No quantitative contribution fractions for asteroids versus comets are derived.</s>
