# Methods and Strategies for Building and Refining Reasoning Models

Reasoning models are emerging as a key specialization trend for LLMs in 2025. This evolution moves beyond familiar patterns like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) or domain-specific fine-tuning. Instead of just injecting knowledge, reasoning specialization aims to cultivate robust, multi-step logical capabilities within the models themselves.![Figure 1: Stages 1-3 are the common steps to developing LLMs. Stage 4 specializes LLMs for specific use cases.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!QwUc!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd6ebc5c9-461f-4d3a-889b-b8ea4e14e5ba_1600x830.png)
Image 1: Stages 1-3 are the common steps to developing LLMs. Stage 4 specializes LLMs for specific use cases. (Source: [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling))

This specialization is tailored for complex tasks like mathematical proofs, logical puzzles, and competitive programming. It does not replace general-purpose LLMs for everyday tasks like generation, summarization, or simple question-answering. However, these advanced reasoning capabilities come with trade-offs. The gains are often accompanied by increased inference latency and cost due to longer, more detailed outputs. There is also a risk of "overthinking" on trivial tasks, where a model might introduce unnecessary complexity. Furthermore, specialization is not a free lunch; improving capability in one area can sometimes degrade fluency or efficiency in others.

Having set the context and roadmap, we will now establish a working definition of a "reasoning model" that you can use to evaluate future systems and research.

## How do we define "reasoning model"?

A reasoning model is an LLM designed to solve multi-step problems by generating intermediate steps or structured "thought" processes. Unlike models that provide a direct answer, reasoning models either explicitly display their thought process or handle it internally. This allows them to perform better at complex tasks like puzzles, coding challenges, and mathematical problems [[19]](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling). For example, asking "How far does a train traveling at 60 mph for 3 hours go?" might get a direct answer from a standard LLM, but a reasoning model would first state the formula `distance = speed × time` before calculating the result.![Figure 2: A regular LLM may only provide a short answer (as shown on the left), whereas reasoning models typically include intermediate steps that reveal part of the thought process. (Note that many LLMs who have not been specifically developed for reasoning tasks can also provide intermediate reasoning steps in their answers.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!8oZo!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff2987079-25f4-45fb-a020-1ac936ed16cb_1424x820.png)
Image 2: A regular LLM may only provide a short answer (as shown on the left), whereas reasoning models typically include intermediate steps that reveal part of the thought process. (Source: [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling))

The spectrum of reasoning is broad. All modern LLMs exhibit some basic reasoning, which can be improved with techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting [[25]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916). However, specialized reasoning models are engineered to achieve exceptional performance on difficult benchmarks, such as math olympiad problems or formal proofs.

The intermediate steps in these models manifest in two primary ways:
1.  **Visible thought traces:** The model generates step-by-step outputs that the user can read and follow.
2.  **Invisible internal iterations:** The model allocates additional computation time at inference to "think" without exposing every token of its process, a technique rumored to be used in models like OpenAI's o1.![Figure 3: "Reasoning" is used at two different levels: 1) processing the input and generating via multiple intermediate steps and 2) providing some sort of reasoning as part of the response to the user.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!DyRP!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F35712d0e-0f40-4855-8d81-4dcea94055ce_1538x810.png)
Image 3: "Reasoning" can refer to the internal process of generating a response or the explicit explanation provided to the user. (Source: [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling))

With a clear definition established, we can now examine the practical question of when deploying these more expensive reasoning models actually makes sense for an AI engineer.

## When should we use reasoning models?

Before diving into the technical details, it is important to know when reasoning models are truly needed. They deliver the highest returns on tasks that require decomposition or self-correction, such as puzzles, advanced mathematics, and competitive coding. However, they are often overkill for summarization, simple factual question-answering, or creative writing, where direct generation is sufficient.

Their practical downsides are significant. The verbose intermediate steps lead to higher latency and token costs [[38]](https://neoteric.eu/blog/gpt-o1-vs-gpt-4o-comparison), [[41]](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/analysis-openai-o1-vs-gpt-4o). This increased verbosity can also frustrate users in conversational settings. Furthermore, there is a risk of "overthinking," where a model might invent unnecessary complications for a straightforward problem that a base model would have solved correctly in a single pass [[31]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18585). This tendency to generate excessive tokens, even for simple problems, is a known side effect of optimizing for long chain-of-thought reasoning [[50]](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/3e22bea3b170f4c2aebb9c48d98ae64d-Paper-Conference.pdf).![Figure 4: The key strengths and weaknesses of reasoning models.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!lnf2!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F46dbe029-ab7d-4278-8dfe-7bc4af79a103_1352x524.png)
Image 4: The key strengths and weaknesses of reasoning models.

Understanding when to deploy reasoning models leads naturally to studying a concrete, open pipeline that demonstrates how such capabilities are created at scale.

## A brief look at the DeepSeek training pipeline

The DeepSeek-R1 series offers a transparent look into a state-of-the-art reasoning model pipeline. It consists of three main variants, each building on the last [[24]](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-r1-recipe-for-o1), [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).![Figure 5: Development process of DeepSeeks three different reasoning models that are discussed in the DeepSeek R1 technical report.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!z-dr!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdb19df56-c5bf-4a0c-aafb-4629a39b13f5_1542x1166.png)
Image 5: The development process of DeepSeek's three reasoning models. (Source: [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

First is **DeepSeek-R1-Zero**, which is created using a "cold-start" pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) process directly from the DeepSeek-V3 base model. This approach bypasses the conventional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage that typically precedes RL. The model learns to reason by being rewarded for correct final answers on verifiable tasks, allowing reasoning traces to emerge naturally without being constrained by human-annotated examples [[4]](https://thelmbook.com/articles#!./DeepSeek-R1.md), [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).

Next is **DeepSeek-R1**, the flagship model. It refines R1-Zero by incorporating SFT stages and additional RL training. It begins with a "cold-start" SFT on a small dataset of human-aligned reasoning traces to improve readability and stability. This is followed by large-scale RL with additional rewards for language consistency to address issues like language mixing that appeared in R1-Zero [[26]](https://transitions.substack.com/p/the-laymans-introduction-to-deepseek), [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).

Finally, the **DeepSeek-R1-Distill** models are smaller, more efficient versions. Their creation is not classical logit-based knowledge distillation. Instead, smaller open-source models like Llama and Qwen are trained via SFT on a large dataset of 800,000 high-quality reasoning outputs generated by the full DeepSeek-R1 model [[11]](https://medium.com/data-science-in-your-pocket/what-are-deepseek-r1-distilled-models-329629968d5d), [[14]](https://www.bentoml.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-deepseek-models-from-v3-to-r1-and-beyond). This transfers the reasoning capabilities to more accessible models.

The DeepSeek pipeline incorporates all four main techniques for building reasoning models, which we will now examine in depth.

## The 4 main ways to build and improve reasoning models

Current techniques for enhancing LLM reasoning can be grouped into four main categories. While the exact workings of frontier models like OpenAI's o1 and o3 remain undisclosed, they are rumored to combine both inference-time and training-time methods [[19]](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling).

### 1) Inference-time scaling

Inference-time scaling refers to improving a model's performance by allocating more computational resources during inference, without altering the model's weights. The core idea is analogous to giving a human more time to think through a difficult problem. This approach is also known as test-time compute scaling, and research shows that performance on complex benchmarks often increases with more compute, whether at training time or test time [[48]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314).

One of the simplest methods is Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, where a phrase like "Let's think step by step" encourages the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps [[25]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916). This method has historical roots in symbolic AI systems, which relied on structured, step-by-step logical deduction to solve problems. CoT prompting elicits a similar pattern of structured thought from modern LLMs, bridging the gap between connectionist and symbolic approaches to reasoning [[51]](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/13/11/1707). This naturally increases the number of generated tokens and, therefore, the inference cost.![Figure 6: An example of classic CoT prompting from the 2022 Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners paper](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!VFAa!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F523eee5e-afb6-4019-a11b-e0a291d2c286_1600x419.png)
Image 6: An example of classic CoT prompting from the 2022 *Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners* paper. (Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916) [[25]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916))

More advanced techniques involve search strategies. These methods generate multiple potential solutions and use a verifier, or a Process Reward Model (PRM), to select the best one. A PRM provides fine-grained, step-level feedback, contrasting with an Outcome Reward Model (ORM), which only judges the final answer. This process-level supervision is crucial for diagnosing intermediate errors and guiding complex reasoning, but it also makes PRMs more resource-intensive to create, as they require correctness labels for every intermediate step, not just the final result [[52]](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08049v3), [[53]](https://www.jonvet.com/blog/llm-test-time-compute). The computational expense of training and running large PRMs has led to research into more efficient alternatives. These include lightweight verifiers that probe a model's internal states to estimate the credibility of a reasoning step without needing a separate, large reward model [[54]](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.06209v3). Common search strategies include:
*   **Best-of-N:** The model generates N full solutions, and a verifier selects the best one.
*   **Beam Search:** The model explores a limited number of top-k solutions at each step, pruning less promising paths.
*   **Lookahead Search / Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS):** This method builds on beam search by performing rollouts to estimate the future value of a particular reasoning step, allowing for more strategic exploration of the solution space [[48]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314).![Figure 7: Different search-based methods rely on a process-reward-based model to select the best answer. Annotated figure from the LLM Test-Time Compute paper](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!YGJO!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5cb10e5a-738b-4c9e-ba65-5850d4793706_1600x919.png)
Image 7: Different search-based methods rely on a process-reward-based model to select the best answer. (Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314) [[48]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314))

Interestingly, the DeepSeek R1 technical report categorized these common inference-time scaling methods under "unsuccessful attempts" during their training process [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). This suggests that their model's ability to generate long reasoning traces is an intrinsic property learned during training, rather than a result of explicit search algorithms at inference. However, this does not preclude DeepSeek from applying these techniques at the application layer to further boost performance.

The high cost of models like OpenAI's o1 compared to GPT-4o is likely due to this heavy use of inference-time compute. The o1 models are slower and more expensive, designed to spend more time "thinking" before responding, which makes them better at complex science, coding, and math problems [[37]](https://www.uctoday.com/unified-communications/chatgpt-4o-vs-o1-which-openai-model-is-best), [[38]](https://neoteric.eu/blog/gpt-o1-vs-gpt-4o-comparison), [[40]](https://francpetracci.medium.com/openai-gpt-o1-api-pricing-a-comprehensive-guide-b93fdaed217c).

### 2) Pure reinforcement learning (RL)

The second approach, pure RL, is exemplified by DeepSeek-R1-Zero. Unlike typical RL pipelines that start with an SFT model, this method applies RL directly to a base model. Instead of using a reward model trained on human preferences, it uses simple, verifiable reward signals [[4]](https://thelmbook.com/articles#!./DeepSeek-R1.md), [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).![Figure 8: The development process of DeepSeek-R1-Zero model.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_9Z-!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa5bb6ecc-7e46-45fe-abff-1eb02e6b0e3a_1556x1162.png)
Image 8: The development process of the DeepSeek-R1-Zero model. (Source: [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

The reward structure in the DeepSeek R1 paper consists of two main components:
*   **Accuracy rewards:** A binary reward (e.g., 1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) based on whether the final answer is correct. This is possible for tasks with deterministic solutions, like math problems or coding challenges with unit tests [[22]](https://thelmbook.com/articles#!./DeepSeek-R1.md), [[23]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z).
*   **Format rewards:** The model is rewarded for following specific formatting rules, such as enclosing its reasoning process within `<think>` and `</think>` tags. This encourages interpretability and structured output without constraining the content of the reasoning itself [[24]](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-r1-recipe-for-o1).

During this pure RL training, the DeepSeek team observed an "Aha moment" [[7]](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a9GR7m4nyBsqjjL8d/deepseek-r1-for-beginners), [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). The model spontaneously began to generate long, complex reasoning traces, including self-reflection and self-correction patterns, without being explicitly taught to do so. For example, it would generate phrases like "Wait, wait. Wait. That's an aha moment I can flag here," before re-evaluating its approach. This emergent behavior was accompanied by a steady increase in the average length of the model's responses during training [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).![Figure 9: A figure from the DeepSeek R1 technical report showing the emergence of the "Aha" moment.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Prn2!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F30f8e37b-ba60-49d2-a95e-9c06b2033ee4_1600x1019.png)
Image 9: An example from the DeepSeek R1 technical report showing the "Aha moment" where the model learns to self-reflect. (Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948))

However, a growing body of research suggests this "Aha moment" is less about RL creating a new capability and more about it amplifying a latent one. Self-reflection and self-correction behaviors appear to emerge during pre-training and are already present, albeit rarely activated, in the base models [[55]](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/the-state-of-llm-reasoning-model-training). Recent studies have shown that these models maintain distinct internal representations for reflective and non-reflective states, even before fine-tuning. This suggests that pure RL acts as a powerful amplifier, making these latent abilities more frequent and reliable, rather than creating them from scratch [[56]](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12217v1). Nonetheless, R1-Zero was the first instance to show that it is possible to develop reasoning capabilities with pure RL, by having the model generate its own intermediate "thinking" steps.

### 3) Supervised finetuning and reinforcement learning (SFT + RL)

The third approach is a hybrid model that combines SFT and RL, as seen in the full DeepSeek-R1 model. This multi-stage process aims to build upon the raw reasoning capabilities developed in R1-Zero while improving stability, readability, and general helpfulness [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). This is because pure RL from a cold start can be unstable and lead to issues like language mixing. A preliminary SFT stage provides a better-aligned starting point, which helps stabilize and accelerate the subsequent RL training [[57]](https://cameronrwolfe.substack.com/p/demystifying-reasoning-models).![Figure 10: The development process of DeepSeek-R1 model.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!19pK!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdf7f99f0-d154-49e5-b60a-4d148e0a61be_1548x1154.png)
Image 10: The development process of the DeepSeek-R1 model. (Source: [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

The pipeline involves several key steps:
1.  **Cold-Start SFT:** The process begins by fine-tuning the base model on a small "cold-start" dataset of a few thousand high-quality reasoning examples. This data is curated to be human-aligned and conversational, which helps stabilize the subsequent RL training and prevents issues like language mixing [[1]](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a9GR7m4nyBsqjjL8d/deepseek-r1-for-beginners), [[25]](https://artgor.medium.com/paper-review-deepseek-r1-incentivizing-reasoning-capability-in-llms-via-reinforcement-learning-edf4343dcf3a). While SFT is a common approach, other methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) have also been explored for this warm-up phase. Using a DPO-based cold-start can sometimes lead to more stable RL convergence, as its objective is more aligned with the preference-based nature of RL [[58]](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25801v3).
2.  **Reasoning-Oriented RL:** The model then undergoes large-scale RL, similar to R1-Zero, using verifiable rewards for math, code, and logic problems. At this stage, a **language consistency reward** is introduced. This reward, calculated as the proportion of words in the target language, penalizes the model for mixing languages (e.g., Chinese and English) in its response. While this slightly degrades performance on some reasoning tasks, it significantly improves readability and user preference [[27]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z).
3.  **Rejection Sampling and General SFT:** To broaden the model's capabilities beyond pure reasoning, rejection sampling is used to generate a large dataset (800K completions) for a final SFT stage. This dataset includes both reasoning problems and general chat queries, helping the model become more of a general-purpose assistant [[24]](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-r1-recipe-for-o1).
4.  **Final RL Polish:** The last step is another round of RL that mixes verifiable reasoning tasks with standard preference tuning on general prompts. This stage uses model-based reward models to improve helpfulness and harmlessness, polishing the model for general use [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).

This staged refinement process allows DeepSeek-R1 to achieve strong performance across a wide range of benchmarks, often comparable to or exceeding that of closed models like OpenAI's o1 [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).![Figure 11: Benchmark comparison of OpenAI O1 and DeepSeek R1 models. Annotated figure from the DeepSeek-R1 technical report](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!22Cm!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff7f73f16-db4e-4047-89b0-823f16cefb33_1556x490.png)
Image 11: Benchmark comparison of OpenAI o1 and DeepSeek R1 models. (Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948))

### 4) Pure supervised finetuning (SFT) and distillation

To recap, inference-time scaling enhances reasoning without changing model weights, pure RL fosters emergent reasoning behaviors from scratch, and the SFT+RL hybrid method refines these capabilities for production readiness. The fourth approach, pure SFT and distillation, offers a more resource-efficient way to create reasoning models by transferring knowledge from a large, powerful model to smaller ones [[14]](https://www.bentoml.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-deepseek-models-from-v3-to-r1-and-beyond).

DeepSeek's distillation process is straightforward: they used their powerful DeepSeek-R1 model to generate 800,000 high-quality reasoning samples. Then, they used this synthetic dataset to fine-tune smaller, existing open-source models like Qwen and Llama. This is different from traditional knowledge distillation, which often involves matching the output logits of the teacher model. Here, the student model simply learns to mimic the final text output (the reasoning trace) of the teacher through standard SFT [[13]](https://redwerk.com/blog/what-is-model-distillation), [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).![Figure 12: The development process of DeepSeek-R1-Distill models.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!xUjE!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7db7c46b-fe67-49f4-9f65-b0e7b7e5ac08_1444x1174.png)
Image 12: The development process of DeepSeek-R1-Distill models. (Source: [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

DeepSeek created these distilled models for two key reasons. First, the full 671B parameter R1 model is computationally expensive and difficult to run on consumer-grade hardware. Distillation makes these powerful reasoning capabilities accessible to a wider audience [[11]](https://medium.com/data-science-in-your-pocket/what-are-deepseek-r1-distilled-models-329629968d5d). Second, this process is much more efficient than training a smaller model from scratch with pure RL [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).

The results are impressive. Even the smallest distilled model, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, surpasses strong non-reasoning baselines like GPT-4o on mathematical benchmarks [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).![Figure 13: Benchmark comparison of distilled versus non-distilled models. Annotated figure from the DeepSeek-R1 technical report](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!XwZe!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Febc749fb-6a79-483f-bcda-b219f284bc09_1168x604.png)
Image 13: Benchmark comparison of distilled versus non-distilled models. (Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948))

The DeepSeek report also provides a direct comparison between distillation and pure RL on a smaller 32B model. They found that while pure RL did improve the model's reasoning, the distilled version performed significantly better across all benchmarks. This suggests that for smaller models, it is more effective to transfer knowledge from a larger, more capable teacher than to try to elicit reasoning from scratch through RL [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).![Figure 14: Benchmark comparison distillation and RL on a smaller 32B model. Annotated figure from the DeepSeek-R1 technical report](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5_5L!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F05514c9f-eb04-496b-bd98-bb4710c65b14_1448x408.png)
Image 14: Benchmark comparison of distillation and RL on a smaller 32B model. (Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948))

This insight is valuable: while advancing the frontier of AI may require massive base models and large-scale RL, distillation is a powerful and economical strategy for disseminating those capabilities. It would have been even more insightful if the comparison also included a model trained with the SFT+RL hybrid approach, or if the RL-trained models were given a "warm-up" with the distilled data before RL.

After dissecting the four techniques and seeing them embodied in DeepSeek-R1, we can step back to evaluate the broader significance and limitations of this release.

## Thoughts about DeepSeek R1

The release of the DeepSeek-R1 models is a significant moment for the AI community. We appreciate the team's commitment to openness, demonstrated by the permissive MIT license and the unusually detailed technical report that accompanies the models [[24]](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-r1-recipe-for-o1). This transparency provides a valuable blueprint for researchers and engineers. Most importantly, the success of R1-Zero shows that pure RL from a cold start can produce emergent reasoning and self-correction, a finding that could accelerate research in this area.

When comparing DeepSeek-R1 to OpenAI's o1, the models show comparable performance on many reasoning benchmarks, particularly in math and coding [[47]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). However, R1 appears to be more inference-efficient, with significantly lower API pricing [[24]](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-r1-recipe-for-o1). This suggests different design philosophies: DeepSeek seems to have invested heavily in a training-heavy approach to bake reasoning into the model, while o1 may lean more on expensive inference-time scaling to achieve its results.

However, a direct comparison is difficult because so much about o1 remains unknown. We do not know its model size, whether it uses a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture, or the exact mix of training techniques employed [[45]](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/what-went-into-training-deepseek-r1). Without these details, attributing performance differences to specific methods is speculative.

There is also some ambiguity around the true training cost. While a figure of around $5.3 million has been associated with the DeepSeek-V3 base model, the incremental cost of the R1-specific RL and SFT stages is estimated to be around $294,000 [[42]](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-deepseek-says-its-hit-ai-model-cost-just-294000-train-2025-09-18), [[43]](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/what-went-into-training-deepseek-r1). The full development cost remains undisclosed, making it hard to assess the total investment required for such a project.

Despite these caveats, DeepSeek-R1 stands as a milestone. It proves that high-level reasoning capabilities need not be the exclusive domain of closed, proprietary labs. By providing an open, powerful reasoning model and a detailed recipe, DeepSeek has accelerated research and development for the entire community.

## Developing reasoning models on a limited budget

The development costs for state-of-the-art reasoning models can be discouraging for teams with limited budgets. However, recent open-source projects have demonstrated that it is possible to achieve impressive reasoning capabilities without massive computational resources.

One practical approach is distillation, as demonstrated by the **Sky-T1** project from UC Berkeley’s Sky Computing Lab. By fine-tuning a 32B parameter model on a carefully curated dataset of 17,000 reasoning examples, the team was able to create a model competitive with early versions of OpenAI's o1 on several benchmarks. The entire training process cost less than $450, showcasing how effective distillation can be when paired with high-quality data [[15]](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/muhammad-ali-masood-phd_researchers-open-source-sky-t1-a-reasoning-activity-7285536292493299712-M8b5), [[17]](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/11/researchers-open-source-sky-t1-a-reasoning-ai-model-that-can-be-trained-for-less-than-450).![Figure 15: Figure from the "Sky-T1: Train your own O1 preview model within $450" article](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Y8HI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8865a313-2326-4f07-a6dc-72cc94cb2ebe_1364x570.png)
Image 15: Benchmark results for the budget-friendly Sky-T1 model. (Source: [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

Even pure RL can be applied at a smaller scale. The **TinyZero** project successfully reproduced DeepSeek R1-Zero's emergent behaviors on a 3B parameter model for under $30 [[20]](https://github.com/Jiayi-Pan/TinyZero). By focusing on specific tasks like the Countdown numbers game, the model learned self-verification and search abilities on its own, reinforcing the idea that cold-start RL can yield useful results even without massive compute [[5]](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-r1/mini-r1-contdown-game), [[21]](https://www.dailycal.org/news/campus/research-and-ideas/campus-researchers-replicate-disruptive-chinese-ai-for-30/article_a1cc5cd0-dee4-11ef-b8ca-171526dfb895.html).![Figure 16: A figure from the TinyZero repository showing that the model is capable of self-verification.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Ykdn!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6111f4b4-cfb9-494c-8390-ec251702914b_1600x955.png)
Image 16: An example from the TinyZero repository showing the model's self-verification capabilities. (Source: [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

Another promising, budget-conscious technique is **journey learning**. This paradigm shifts from "shortcut learning," where models are only trained on correct solution paths, to a more holistic approach. In journey learning, the training data includes the entire problem-solving process: incorrect paths, self-corrections, and reflections, culminating in the final success. The "O1 Replication Journey" paper found that training a model on just 327 examples of these full "journeys" improved performance on the MATH dataset by over 8% compared to training on only the correct "shortcut" solutions [[49]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18982).![Figure 17: Journey learning, as opposed to traditional shortcut learning, includes wrong solutions paths in the SFT data. Annotated figure from the O1 Replication Journey: A Strategic Progress Report – Part 1](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TxCO!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7a0bfcd0-6d93-4c91-a0d6-28178839b7cf_1492x724.png)
Image 17: Journey learning includes incorrect solution paths in the SFT data, unlike traditional shortcut learning. (Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18982) [[49]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18982))

By exposing the model to the process of making and correcting mistakes, journey learning reinforces self-correction mechanisms. This contrasts with models trained only on "golden" solutions, which can become brittle and fail when they encounter novel errors. However, journey learning is not without its failure modes. If the training data is dominated by error paths, it can lead to unstable convergence or even cause the model to persistently generate incorrect answers, as the fine-tuning objective conflicts with the base model's prior knowledge [[59]](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01624v1). There is also a risk of overfitting to specific types of errors in the data, which can paradoxically increase the frequency of certain mistakes while improving overall accuracy [[60]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.605.pdf).

Looking ahead, the most effective budget-friendly strategies will likely be hybrids. By combining insights from these projects—using high-quality distilled data like Sky-T1, fostering emergent behaviors with small-scale RL like TinyZero, and building robustness with journey learning—teams can create powerful reasoning models that balance cost, performance, and reliability.

## Conclusion

We have explored the four primary approaches to building and improving reasoning models. Inference-time scaling offers a way to boost performance without any training cost but comes at a high serving cost. Pure RL, as demonstrated by DeepSeek-R1-Zero, is a powerful research tool for unlocking emergent behaviors like self-correction. The SFT+RL hybrid method provides a production-ready blueprint that combines the stability of supervised data with the exploratory power of RL. Finally, distillation offers an efficient method for transferring capabilities to smaller models, though its performance is ultimately derivative of the teacher model.

The future of reasoning models will likely be a hybrid one. The most capable systems will probably combine robust SFT+RL training pipelines with sophisticated inference-time scaling techniques. This is precisely the pattern we suspect underlies models like o1 and will likely define future o3-class systems. The impact of these techniques extends beyond benchmarks. In robotics, reasoning models are being adapted to decompose high-level commands into executable steps for navigation and manipulation, allowing autonomous systems to handle complex, long-horizon tasks [[61]](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-2688/6/7/158). Similarly, in finance, LLMs are being deployed as trading agents that can express their reasoning in natural language, creating new possibilities for algorithmic trading and market analysis [[62]](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10789v1).

For AI engineers, the key takeaway is strategic. The choice of which technique to use should be driven by concrete constraints: budget, performance targets, latency requirements, and the need for innovation. Instead of defaulting to the largest, most expensive proprietary model, a careful analysis of the task's complexity and the available resources can lead to a more optimal solution. By understanding the trade-offs between these four approaches, you can design and build reasoning systems that are not only powerful but also practical and efficient.
