## Context of the Article

### What We Are Planning to Share

- We are producing a standalone article that introduces AI engineers to the 2024 New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness and the evidentiary shift that now supports the realistic possibility of phenomenal consciousness in insects, cephalopods, decapod crustaceans, and many other invertebrates.
- The article opens with bumblebee play behavior as a concrete hook, states the Declaration’s core claim, distinguishes phenomenal consciousness (Nagel’s “what it is like”) from higher-order self-awareness, lists the interdisciplinary signatories, contrasts prior mammalian-centric assumptions with the new consensus, and explicitly differentiates shared animal subjective experience from today’s large language models.
- Subsequent sections chronicle the 10–15 years of behavioral evidence (octopus pain, cuttlefish episodic-like memory, fish mirror-test variants, bee play, crayfish anxiety-like states modulated by anxiolytics), explain the move from informal agreement to public declaration, compare the 2012 Cambridge and 2024 New York declarations, reject the neural-complexity barrier, illustrate distributed versus centralized nervous systems, and show that cerebral-cortex-like functions can be performed by radically different architectures.
- The final section translates the consensus into ethical obligations, practical tensions with insects, laboratory welfare gaps, UK policy precedents, a cautionary parallel to machine consciousness, and a call to redirect existing lab infrastructure toward nematodes and fruit flies.
- A Mermaid timeline diagram is required to visualize the widening circle of species between the two declarations; all content remains 100 % theoretical, linking directly to prior lessons on Nagel’s bat and the Cambridge Declaration while previewing future lessons on AI moral patienthood.

### Why We Think It's Valuable

- Seeing how the evidentiary bar for consciousness has been lowered in biology supplies humility and a richer conceptual vocabulary when the same standards are later applied to large language models and tool-using agents.

## Section 1 - Introduction

- Open with the concrete experimental finding in 2022 that bumblebees voluntarily manipulate wooden balls in a dedicated arena even when satiated and under no survival pressure, demonstrating behavior fully decoupled from foraging, reproduction, or immediate reward; use this as the paradigmatic example that first convinces the reader that complex affective states can exist in tiny brains.
- Draw the connection between the above finding about bumblebees with 2024 New York Declaration. Narrate the formation, in the last decades, of the broad consensus among scientists on the consciousness in animals similar to humans, and then the recent acknowledgement of consciouness in animals distant from humans. Next, state the core claim of the New York Declaration, which embraces the recent view on the widespread consciousness among animals, verbatim, and follow with a one-sentence conclusion of the declaration: there is a realistic possibility of phenomenal consciousness in all vertebrates and in at least three invertebrate clades—cephalopods, decapod crustaceans, and insects—emphasizing that the declaration deliberately uses “realistic possibility” rather than absolute certainty.
- Use a paragraph to show some background information of New York Declaration, including where and when it was released, and who spearheaded the declaration and the proeminent people who signed it (including scientists, psycologists and philsophers).
- Define phenomenal consciousness strictly via Nagel’s “what it is like” criterion by including the original quote from his influential 1974 essay, contrasting it with richer capacities such as self-awareness or metacognition that the Declaration does not claim for these animals.
- Draw an explicit contrast between the behavioral and physiological indicators of shared animal subjective experience and the current limitations of large language models such as ChatGPT, which lack analogous play, pain trade-offs, or anxiety-like states despite impressive linguistic performance. Complement the contrast with quotes from researchers like Anil Seth on the declaration and the shared commonalities in consciousness between the humans and other animals.
- Include a Mermaid diagram that renders a conceptual timeline contrasting the 2012 Cambridge Declaration with the 2024 New York Declaration and the widening circle of species now considered possibly conscious; the diagram must show a horizontal arrow labeled with years, concentric circles expanding outward from “mammals & birds (2012)” to “all vertebrates + cephalopods, decapods, insects (2024),” and callouts noting the shift from “consciousness” to “realistic possibility of phenomenal consciousness.”
- Transition to Section 2: Conclude the introduction by noting that the 2024 Declaration is the formal culmination of a rapid accumulation of behavioral evidence gathered over the last 10–15 years; signal that the next section examines that evidence in detail, explains why the field moved to a public declaration, and dismantles the old neural-complexity barrier.

- **Section length:** 600 words

## Section 2 - A Growing Awareness

- Catalog the rapid accumulation of behavioral evidence over the last 10–15 years by presenting, in sequence and citing original results: (1) octopuses exhibiting directed wound-grooming and conditioned place aversion after injury, (2) cuttlefish passing episodic-like memory tests that require integrating what–where–when information, (3) zebra fish showing mirror-test variants that include mark-directed behavior after training, (4) bumblebees engaging in object play with wooden balls, (5) fruit flies have distinct sleep patterns and (6) crayfish displaying anxiety-like states that are reduced by human anxiolytic drugs such as benzodiazepines. Do NOT generate any mermaid graph for this presentation of behavioral evidence in sequence!
- Explain why the field moved from informal agreement among specialists to a public declaration: the accumulating data created a scientific consensus that needed to be communicated clearly to policymakers, funding bodies, and the broader scientific community to influence legislation and research priorities. Quote experts like Jeff Sebo about the public opinion on consciousness among animals and the intention of the declaration.
- Compare wording and scope between the two declarations by quoting key phrases: the 2012 Cambridge Declaration asserts that mammals and birds possess the neurological substrates for consciousness, whereas the 2024 New York Declaration widens the circle to all vertebrates plus the three invertebrate clades and deliberately adopts the more cautious epistemic phrasing “realistic possibility of phenomenal consciousness.” Also, quote remarks by experts on the caution regarding the new declaration and the interpretation of the complex behaviors exhibited by animals.
- Reject the hypothesis that having brains and nervous systems very different from those of humans is a barrier to consciousness by two examples: 1. contrasting the bee’s roughly one million richly arborized neurons—sufficient for play, navigation, and social learning—with the human brain’s 86 billion neurons, showing that absolute neuron count is not a prerequisite for subjective experience;  2. Illustrate distributed versus centralized nervous systems with the concrete example of an octopus arm that, once severed, continues to explore, manipulate objects, and exhibit avoidance behavior for minutes to hours, demonstrating that sophisticated sensory-motor integration can occur without constant centralized control.
- Demonstrate that a cerebral cortex is not required by detailing how fish, birds, reptiles, and invertebrates achieve analogous computational functions through entirely different neural architectures—such as the avian pallium or the octopus vertical lobe—thereby removing the last architectural objection to invertebrate consciousness. Use direct quotes from experts like Kristin Andrews and Peter Godfrey-Smith on the relation between brain structure and consciousness.
- Transition to Section 3: Summarize that the evidentiary and architectural arguments have now shifted the scientific default; pivot to the downstream ethical obligations, practical tensions, and laboratory-policy implications that follow once we accept the realistic possibility of consciousness in these clades.

- **Section length:** 850 words

## Section 3 - Mindful Relations

- Argue that once phenomenal consciousness is considered realistically possible, ethical obligations extend beyond the mere absence of pain to positive environmental enrichment that permits animals to express species-typical behaviors such as exploration, play, and social interaction. Reinforce the argument with original quotes by experts like Sebo.
- Examine practical tensions that arise when consciousness is granted to insects: crop pests, disease vectors, and the structural inevitability of antagonistic human–insect relations mean that welfare considerations cannot eliminate all harm, forcing explicit trade-off reasoning rather than blanket avoidance. Quote experts like Godfrey-Smith on the practical tensions to other animals.
- Highlight the overlooked welfare gap in laboratory research by noting the routine use of Drosophila and other insects in genetic, developmental, and neuroscience experiments without any sentience-specific protocols, in contrast to the stricter oversight now applied to cephalopods and decapods. Quote experts on this welfare gap in research.
- Cite the UK legislative precedent in which sentience reviews led to explicit inclusion of cephalopods and decapod crustaceans in animal-welfare statutes, showing a concrete policy translation pathway that could be extended to insects.
- Draw a cautionary parallel to machine consciousness by reminding the reader that today’s large language models and agentic architectures remain unlikely to be conscious on the same evidentiary standards, yet the rapid shift in the animal literature counsels epistemic humility when evaluating future AI systems. Quote experts on the issue of possible AI consciousness.
- Quote Andrew's call to redirect existing laboratory infrastructure—already stocked with nematodes and fruit flies on every campus—toward targeted consciousness research programs that could further expand the awareness of the scope of consciousness. Then, further discuss the future prospects of research into consciousness in even wider range of animals.
