## Context of the Article

### What We Are Planning to Share

We are planning to share a detailed examination of entropic gravity as an emergent explanation for gravitational attraction, framing it as a statistical outcome of entropy maximization in underlying microscopic thermal systems or qubit-like degrees of freedom rather than a fundamental force. The article contrasts historical mechanical "push" models from the Newton era and Jacobson's thermodynamic derivation of general relativity against the holographic principle as the dominant emergence route. It details two recent qubit-based models from Carney et al. 2025 that reproduce Newtonian attraction—one via localized order pockets and the other via variable energy capacity—then critiques their ad-hoc tuning, limited scope to the weak-field regime, failure to capture the equivalence principle, and challenges in strong-field regimes. The piece concludes by connecting these ideas to quantum superposition collapse and prospects for tabletop experiments that could distinguish statistical gravity from immutable laws, while referencing Verlinde 2010 on emergent gravity and Jacobson 1995 on thermodynamics of spacetime.

### Why We Think It's Valuable

We think it is valuable because entropic gravity is a meaningful alternative approach to theorize the emergence of space-time and gravitation from microscopic components besides the mainstream holographic approach, as it prompts conceptual questions about gravity and open up new experimental directions in spite of its weaknesses.

## Section 3 - Apparent Attraction

- Concisely state Carney and his co-authors put forward two models inspired Jacobson;s approach to model the emergence of gravity out of microscopic components.
- Present the first qubit-lattice model from Carney et al. 2025: masses embedded in a lattice of qubits polarize nearby qubits, creating localized low-entropy "order pockets" where the microscopic degrees of freedom become more correlated or aligned. Include direct quotes by Carney to explain the first model.  
- Explain entropy maximization as the driver that pushes the masses closer together, thereby minimizing the total volume of these ordered regions and reducing the overall entropic cost, so that the observed attraction is an epiphenomenon of the system's tendency to increase global entropy.
- Detail how the lattice model naturally yields inverse-square distance dependence without extra assumptions: the polarization effect falls off with distance, causing the entropic force (gradient of free energy) to reproduce the Newtonian 1/r² law as a direct statistical outcome.
- Describe the second nonlocal-qubit model from Carney et al. 2025 that allows qubits to be arbitrarily distant from one another, thereby capturing the instantaneous action-at-a-distance character of Newtonian gravity without requiring a local lattice medium.
- Elaborate on the variable per-qubit energy capacity mechanism: when two masses approach, the energy capacity of each qubit decreases, forcing any fixed total energy to spread across a larger number of qubits; this spreading increases the number of accessible microstates and thus raises entropy, driving the masses together to maximize entropy.
- Clarify that both models treat observed attraction as an epiphenomenon of underlying qubit buffering and the universal tendency toward higher entropy, with the first relying on local order pockets and the second on nonlocal energy-capacity modulation, both connecting to the statistical mechanics view of entropy as disorder or missing information.

## Section 4 - Strengths and Weaknesses

- Critique the ad-hoc construction of both models, which require fine-tuned coupling strengths between masses and qubits together with specific choices of lattice spacing or nonlocal connectivity that lack independent microscopic evidence or derivation from a more fundamental theory.
