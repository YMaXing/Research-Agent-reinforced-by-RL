# Methods and Strategies for Building and Refining Reasoning Models

Reasoning models are set to be the key LLM specialization trend for 2025. This goes beyond the patterns we already know, like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and domain fine-tuning. Instead of just injecting knowledge, reasoning specialization targets the emergence of robust, multi-step logical capabilities. This new layer of specialization focuses on teaching models *how* to think, not just *what* to know.![Image 1: Stages 1-3 are the common steps to developing LLMs. Stage 4 specializes LLMs for specific use cases.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd6ebc5c9-461f-4d3a-889b-b8ea4e14e5ba_1600x830.png)
Image 1: Stages 1-3 are the common steps to developing LLMs. Stage 4 specializes LLMs for specific use cases. (Source [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling))

This specialization is aimed at complex multi-step tasks like mathematical proofs, logical puzzles, and competitive programming. It does not replace or diminish the utility of general-purpose LLMs for everyday generation, summarization, or simple question-answering. However, these reasoning gains come with drawbacks. They often introduce higher inference latency and cost due to longer outputs, a risk of overthinking simple tasks, and trade-offs where improving one capability can degrade another. Understanding this balance is essential for any AI engineer looking to build effective and efficient systems.

In this article, we will explain the meaning of a "reasoning model," discuss its advantages and disadvantages, outline the methodology behind DeepSeek R1, describe the four main approaches to building and improving these models, share our thoughts on the LLM landscape following the DeepSeek releases, and provide tips for developing reasoning models on a tight budget.

Having set the context and roadmap, we will now establish a working definition of "reasoning model" that you can use to evaluate future systems and research.

## How do we define "reasoning model"?

A reasoning model is one that generates multi-step intermediate thinking for complex queries. This thinking can be explicit, appearing as token traces the user can read, or hidden, involving internal iterations that are not exposed. This contrasts with the direct factual recall or single-pass pattern matching that is sufficient for simpler prompts. For example, asking "What is the capital of France?" requires a simple fact retrieval. In contrast, a question like "If a train travels at 60 mph for 3 hours, how far does it go?" requires breaking the problem down into a formula (distance = speed × time) and then performing the calculation.![Image 2: A regular LLM may only provide a short answer (as shown on the left), whereas reasoning models typically include intermediate steps that reveal part of the thought process. (Note that many LLMs who have not been specifically developed for reasoning tasks can also provide intermediate reasoning steps in their answers.)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff2987079-25f4-45fb-a020-1ac936ed16cb_1424x820.png)
Image 2: A regular LLM may only provide a short answer, whereas reasoning models typically include intermediate steps that reveal part of the thought process. (Source [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling))

All modern LLMs exhibit some level of reasoning, which can be improved with techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting [[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916). However, specialized reasoning models are designed to excel at difficult benchmarks, such as math olympiad problems, formal proofs, or novel puzzle-solving. This spectrum of capability means that while any powerful LLM can "reason" to some extent, a true reasoning model is optimized for reliability and accuracy on tasks that are unsolvable with a single pass.

This specialization shows up in two main ways. The first is through **visible thought traces**, where the model outputs its step-by-step reasoning process for the user to see. This transparency allows for verification and debugging. The second is through **invisible internal iterations**, like those rumored to be in OpenAI's o1, where the model allocates more computation time at inference to "think" without showing every step. This means "reasoning" happens at two levels: the internal process of generating a response and the final output presented to the user. Both methods aim to improve the final answer's quality by dedicating more computational effort to the problem-solving process.![Image 3: "Reasoning" is used at two different levels: 1) processing the input and generating via multiple intermediate steps and 2) providing some sort of reasoning as part of the response to the user.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F35712d0e-0f40-4855-8d81-4dcea94055ce_1538x810.png)
Image 3: "Reasoning" can refer to the internal multi-step generation process or the explicit reasoning steps shown in the final output. (Source [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling))

With a clear definition established, we can now examine the practical question of when deploying these more expensive reasoning models makes sense for an AI engineer.

## When should we use reasoning models?

Before diving into the technical details, it is important to know when to use reasoning models. They deliver the highest returns on puzzles, advanced mathematics, competitive coding, and problems that require decomposition or self-correction. For tasks like summarization, simple factual question-answering, or creative writing, they are often overkill, introducing unnecessary complexity and cost.

The practical downsides are notable. The verbose intermediate steps lead to higher latency and token costs, making them less suitable for real-time applications. This increased verbosity can also frustrate users in conversational settings who are looking for a quick answer. Furthermore, there is a risk of "overthinking," where a model invents unnecessary complications for a straightforward problem that a general-purpose model would solve correctly in one pass. Choosing the right tool for the job is essential.![Image 4: The key strengths and weaknesses of reasoning models.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1352,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F46dbe029-ab7d-4278-8dfe-7bc4af79a103_1352x524.png)
Image 4: The key strengths and weaknesses of reasoning models. (Source [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling))

Understanding when to deploy reasoning models leads naturally to studying a concrete, open pipeline that demonstrates how such capabilities are created at scale.

## A brief look at the DeepSeek training pipeline

The DeepSeek-R1 series provides an excellent case study on how reasoning models are built. It includes three main variants, each representing a different stage or approach to developing reasoning capabilities [[2]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z), [[3]](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a9GR7m4nyBsqjjL8d/deepseek-r1-for-beginners). This pipeline offers a transparent look into the engineering decisions that shape a state-of-the-art reasoning model, from its initial conception to its final, distilled forms.![Image 5: Development process of DeepSeeks three different reasoning models that are discussed in the DeepSeek R1 technical report.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdb19df56-c5bf-4a0c-aafb-4629a39b13f5_1542x1166.png)
Image 5: Development process of DeepSeek's three different reasoning models that are discussed in the DeepSeek R1 technical report. (Source [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

First is **DeepSeek-R1-Zero**, which was created using a "cold-start" pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) process directly from the DeepSeek-V3 base model. This approach skips the conventional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage that usually precedes RL, allowing reasoning traces to emerge organically. This requires strong, verifiable reward signals, as there is no human-annotated data to guide the initial learning. The model learns to solve problems through trial and error, guided only by whether its final answer is correct. This departure from the standard SFT+RLHF paradigm is significant because it suggests that reasoning can be incentivized without human-provided examples of thought processes.

Next is **DeepSeek-R1**, the flagship reasoning model. It builds on R1-Zero by incorporating additional SFT stages and further RL training. This multi-stage refinement process improves performance, stability, and the readability of the reasoning traces, making it more suitable for production use. It represents a more traditional, polished approach to creating a user-friendly model that still retains the powerful reasoning abilities discovered by R1-Zero.

Finally, there are the **DeepSeek-R1-Distill** models. These are smaller models that inherit the capabilities of the larger R1 model. This is not classical logit-based knowledge distillation. Instead, it involves using the high-quality CoT outputs generated by R1 as a dataset for supervised fine-tuning on smaller, more efficient models. This makes powerful reasoning accessible without the need for massive computational resources, democratizing access to advanced AI.

The DeepSeek pipeline incorporates all four main techniques for building reasoning models, which we will now explore in detail.

## The 4 main ways to build and improve reasoning models

Current techniques for enhancing LLM reasoning can be grouped into four main categories. While the exact workings of proprietary models like OpenAI's o1 and o3 remain undisclosed, they are rumored to combine both inference-time and training-time methods.

### 1) Inference-time scaling

Inference-time scaling refers to increasing the computational resources used during inference to improve a model's performance. It is analogous to giving a human extra time to think about a difficult problem. This is also known as test-time compute scaling [[4]](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40797/44758). This approach is powerful because it can be applied to any existing LLM without retraining, making it a flexible way to boost performance on demand.

One of the simplest methods is **CoT prompting**, where a simple phrase like "think step by step" encourages the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps [[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916). This small change can lead to large improvements in accuracy on complex problems by forcing the model to break them down.![Image 6: An example of classic CoT prompting from the 2022 Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners paper](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F523eee5e-afb6-4019-a11b-e0a291d2c286_1600x419.png)
Image 6: An example of classic CoT prompting from the 2022 *Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners* paper [[1]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916).

More advanced techniques involve search strategies. These include **majority voting**, where multiple responses are generated and the most common answer is selected, and **beam search**, which explores multiple potential response paths simultaneously. Other methods, like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), use a Process Reward Model (PRM) to evaluate and select the most promising reasoning steps during generation [[5]](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling). A PRM provides feedback at each step of the reasoning process, allowing for more fine-grained guidance than a simple final-answer check. These models are crucial for many inference-time scaling techniques, as they provide the signal needed to distinguish good reasoning paths from bad ones [[29]](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/state-of-llm-reasoning-and-inference-scaling).![Image 7: Different search-based methods rely on a process-reward-based model to select the best answer. Annotated figure from the LLM Test-Time Compute paper](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5cb10e5a-738b-4c9e-ba65-5850d4793706_1600x919.png)
Image 7: Different search-based methods rely on a process-reward-based model to select the best answer. (Source [Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314))

Interestingly, the DeepSeek-R1 technical report categorizes common inference-time scaling methods like PRM-based search and MCTS under "unsuccessful attempts" [[6]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). This suggests that their model's reasoning capabilities are primarily baked in during training rather than being heavily reliant on complex inference-time search. However, it is plausible that DeepSeek could still apply these techniques at the application layer to further boost performance. The report's finding may indicate that for their specific architecture and training setup, the computational overhead of these search methods did not justify the performance gains during the large-scale RL process.

The higher cost of models like OpenAI's o1 and o3 compared to GPT-4o might be partially explained by their use of inference-time scaling [[7]](https://www.uctoday.com/unified-communications/chatgpt-4o-vs-o1-which-openai-model-is-best), [[8]](https://neoteric.eu/blog/gpt-o1-vs-gpt-4o-comparison). These models are designed to "think longer" before responding, which requires more computational power and results in higher latency and cost [[9]](https://francpetracci.medium.com/openai-gpt-o1-api-pricing-a-comprehensive-guide-b93fdaed217c), [[10]](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/analysis-openai-o1-vs-gpt-4o). For instance, GPT-o1's input costs are six times higher than GPT-4o's, and its output costs are also six times more expensive [[8]](https://neoteric.eu/blog/gpt-o1-vs-gpt-4o-comparison). This is a direct reflection of the additional computation being performed at inference time to improve the quality and reliability of the final answer.

### 2) Pure reinforcement learning (RL)

Pure RL, as demonstrated by DeepSeek-R1-Zero, involves applying RL directly to a base model without any prior SFT. This approach differs from typical RLHF pipelines, which start with a model already fine-tuned on human demonstrations. It represents a more "from-scratch" method of teaching a model to reason.![Image 8: The development process of DeepSeek-R1-Zero model.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa5bb6ecc-7e46-45fe-abff-1eb02e6b0e3a_1556x1162.png)
Image 8: The development process of the DeepSeek-R1-Zero model. (Source [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

Instead of a reward model trained on human preferences, this method uses verifiable rewards. In the R1 paper, two main types of rule-based rewards were used:
*   **Accuracy rewards**: These provide a positive signal if the model's final answer is correct. This is possible in domains like math and coding, where answers can be automatically verified [[11]](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-r1-recipe-for-o1), [[12]](https://thelmbook.com/articles#!./DeepSeek-R1.md). The reward is binary: 1 for correct, 0 for incorrect. This clear, objective feedback is crucial for the model to learn effective problem-solving strategies without human guidance.
*   **Format rewards**: These incentivize the model to follow specific formatting rules, such as enclosing its reasoning process within `<think>` and `</think>` tags, which helps with stability and interpretability [[11]](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-r1-recipe-for-o1), [[13]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z). This ensures the model's output can be reliably parsed by downstream systems and allows researchers to analyze the model's thought process.

A key finding from the DeepSeek-R1 paper is the emergence of an **"Aha moment"** during training [[3]](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a9GR7m4nyBsqjjL8d/deepseek-r1-for-beginners), [[14]](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-r1/mini-r1-contdown-game). Around the midpoint of RL training, the model spontaneously starts generating long, self-reflective reasoning traces and self-correction patterns, even for simple questions. This was the first demonstration that complex reasoning capabilities could emerge from pure RL without being explicitly taught through supervised data [[6]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). The model learns to allocate more "thinking time" to problems, re-evaluating its own steps with phrases like "Wait, wait. Wait," which is a hallmark of advanced reasoning.![Image 9: A figure from the DeepSeek R1 technical report showing the emergence of the "Aha" moment.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F30f8e37b-ba60-49d2-a95e-9c06b2033ee4_1600x1019.png)
Image 9: A figure from the DeepSeek R1 technical report showing the emergence of the "Aha" moment. (Source [[6]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948))

### 3) Supervised finetuning and reinforcement learning (SFT + RL)

The hybrid SFT+RL approach, used to create the final DeepSeek-R1 model, is a more refined and production-ready method. It combines the stability of supervised learning with the exploratory power of reinforcement learning, creating a balanced and powerful training pipeline [[3]](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a9GR7m4nyBsqjjL8d/deepseek-r1-for-beginners). This multi-stage process is designed to produce a model that is not only a strong reasoner but also well-aligned with human expectations for readability and general helpfulness.![Image 10: The development process of DeepSeek-R1 model.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdf7f99f0-d154-49e5-b60a-4d148e0a61be_1548x1154.png)
Image 10: The development process of the DeepSeek-R1 model. (Source [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

The process involves several stages:
1.  **Cold-Start Data Generation**: A small dataset of a few thousand high-quality, human-aligned reasoning traces is created. This data is used to give the base model an initial ability to generate coherent thought processes, avoiding the "chaotic start" often seen in pure RL [[15]](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/the-training-of-deepseek-r1-and-ways-to-use-it). This initial SFT step provides a solid foundation for the more complex RL phase, ensuring the model already has a basic template for reasoning.
2.  **Reasoning-Oriented RL**: The model is then trained with RL using verifiable rewards, similar to the R1-Zero process. An important addition at this stage is a **language consistency reward**, which penalizes the model for mixing languages in its output. While this can slightly reduce raw accuracy, it improves readability and user preference, which is essential for a production model [[16]](https://artgor.medium.com/paper-review-deepseek-r1-incentivizing-reasoning-capability-in-llms-via-reinforcement-learning-edf4343dcf3a), [[17]](https://transitions.substack.com/p/the-laymans-introduction-to-deepseek). This reward helps to create a more polished and user-friendly experience.
3.  **Iterative Refinement**: The process iterates through multiple stages of SFT and RL, using techniques like rejection sampling to filter for high-quality outputs. This staged refinement leads to a model that is both a strong reasoner and well-aligned with human preferences, achieving state-of-the-art performance on various benchmarks [[6]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). This iterative loop allows the model to continuously improve, absorbing the best strategies discovered during RL back into a more stable, supervised format, while also incorporating general instruction-following and safety data.![Image 11: Benchmark comparison of OpenAI O1 and DeepSeek R1 models. Annotated figure from the DeepSeek-R1 technical report](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff7f73f16-db4e-4047-89b0-823f16cefb33_1556x490.png)
Image 11: Benchmark comparison of OpenAI O1 and DeepSeek R1 models. (Source [DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948))

### 4) Pure supervised finetuning (SFT) and distillation

Inference-time scaling offers a way to improve reasoning without any training, but at a high serving cost. Pure RL unlocks emergent behaviors but can be unstable. The SFT+RL hybrid provides a production-ready blueprint but is resource-intensive. The fourth approach, pure SFT and distillation, offers an efficient way to transfer reasoning capabilities to smaller models.

DeepSeek's approach to distillation is to train smaller, open-source models (like Qwen and Llama) on a high-quality dataset of 800,000 CoT examples generated by the larger R1 model [[18]](https://medium.com/data-science-in-your-pocket/what-are-deepseek-r1-distilled-models-329629968d5d). This differs from traditional distillation, which often involves matching the output distributions (logits) of the teacher model. Here, it is purely supervised fine-tuning on the teacher's outputs, which is a simpler and more direct way to transfer knowledge [[19]](https://redwerk.com/blog/what-is-model-distillation).![Image 12: The development process of DeepSeek-R1-Distill models.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1444,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7db7c46b-fe67-49f4-9f65-b0e7b7e5ac08_1444x1174.png)
Image 12: The development process of DeepSeek-R1-Distill models. (Source [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-reasoning-llms))

DeepSeek developed these distilled models for two main reasons: accessibility and efficiency. Large models like R1 are impractical for many to run, requiring substantial hardware. Distilled models make powerful reasoning accessible on consumer-grade devices, democratizing access to advanced AI capabilities [[18]](https://medium.com/data-science-in-your-pocket/what-are-deepseek-r1-distilled-models-329629968d5d), [[20]](https://www.bentoml.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-deepseek-models-from-v3-to-r1-and-beyond).![Image 13: Benchmark comparison of distilled versus non-distilled models. Annotated figure from the DeepSeek-R1 technical report](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1168,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Febc749fb-6a79-483f-bcda-b219f284bc09_1168x604.png)
Image 13: Benchmark comparison of distilled versus non-distilled models. (Source [DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948))

The DeepSeek report also provides valuable insights into the effectiveness of distillation versus pure RL on smaller models. They trained a Qwen-32B model with pure RL (Qwen2.5-32B-Zero) and compared it to a distilled version of the same size. The distilled model substantially outperformed the RL-trained one across all benchmarks [[6]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948).![Image 14: Benchmark comparison distillation and RL on a smaller 32B model. Annotated figure from the DeepSeek-R1 technical report](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1448,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F05514c9f-eb04-496b-bd98-bb4710c65b14_1448x408.png)
Image 14: Benchmark comparison distillation and RL on a smaller 32B model. (Source [DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948))

This suggests that for smaller models, directly transferring the reasoning patterns discovered by a larger, more capable model through distillation is more effective than trying to elicit those patterns from scratch with RL. The emergent reasoning behaviors seen in large models like R1-Zero may not appear at smaller scales, making distillation a more practical path for creating efficient, yet powerful, reasoning models [[20]](https://www.bentoml.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-deepseek-models-from-v3-to-r1-and-beyond). The table above could have been more useful if it had included additional comparisons, such as the performance of the original Qwen-32B-Base model or a standard instruction-tuned version, to provide a clearer baseline for how much improvement each method provides. It would also be interesting to see a comparison with a model trained with SFT on human-annotated reasoning data, to quantify the value of teacher-generated data.

After dissecting the four techniques and seeing them embodied in DeepSeek-R1, we will step back to evaluate the release's broader significance and limitations.

## Thoughts about DeepSeek R1

The release of the DeepSeek-R1 models is a notable moment for open-source AI. We appreciate the permissive MIT license, the unusually detailed technical report, and the demonstration that pure RL from a cold start can produce emergent reasoning without supervised warm-up. This level of transparency is rare and provides immense value to the research community, offering a clear blueprint for others to build upon.

When comparing DeepSeek-R1 to OpenAI's o1, the benchmarks show comparable quality on math, coding, and reasoning tasks. However, R1 appears to have superior inference efficiency, suggesting a different architectural or training philosophy [[11]](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/deepseek-r1-recipe-for-o1). This could mean that DeepSeek's approach is more training-heavy, baking reasoning capabilities directly into the model's weights, while o1 may lean more on expensive inference-time scaling to achieve its performance.

However, any direct comparison is limited by the secrecy surrounding o1. We do not know its model size, whether it uses a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture, or the exact mix of training techniques. Differences in the strength of the base models also make it difficult to attribute performance gaps to specific methods. A direct comparison is unwarranted until more details about o1 are disclosed.

The discussion around training costs also needs nuance. The DeepSeek team has never disclosed the exact GPU hours or development cost for R1. The widely cited $294,000 figure refers to the total cost of the post-training stages for R1, including R1-Zero and SFT data creation [[6]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). This builds upon the DeepSeek-V3 base model, for which the training cost is a separate, much larger figure, likely around $6 million [[20]](https://www.bentoml.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-deepseek-models-from-v3-to-r1-and-beyond). The true incremental cost of reasoning specialization is therefore much lower than the total cost of creating the model from scratch, but it is not a complete picture.

Despite these caveats, DeepSeek-R1 stands as a milestone for open-weight reasoning models. It proves that high-level reasoning capabilities need not be the exclusive domain of closed labs, and it has accelerated research into all four of the techniques we have discussed.

## Developing reasoning models on a limited budget

The cost of developing large-scale reasoning models is discouraging for many researchers and engineers. However, recent open-source projects have demonstrated that it is possible to achieve impressive results on a limited budget, making advanced AI more accessible. These projects provide practical blueprints for creating capable reasoning models without the need for massive-scale resources.

The **Sky-T1** project from UC Berkeley's Sky Computing Lab is a prime example of distillation's practicality [[21]](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/11/researchers-open-source-sky-t1-a-reasoning-ai-model-that-can-be-trained-for-less-than-450). By carefully curating a dataset of 17,000 samples and fine-tuning an existing model, the team developed a 32B parameter reasoning model for under $450 [[22]](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/muhammad-ali-masood-phd_researchers-open-source-sky-t1-a-reasoning-activity-7285536292493299712-M8b5), [[23]](https://ivan.vlaevski.com/ai-models-go-cheaper-novasky-t1-sets-a-new-standard). Sky-T1 is competitive with early versions of OpenAI's o1 on several reasoning benchmarks, illustrating how effectively distillation can be scaled down [[24]](https://www.technology.org/2025/01/14/sky-t1-open-source-ai-model-for-advanced-reasoning-you-can-train-for-less-than-450), [[25]](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2025/01/15/uc-berkeley-announces-sky-t1-32b-open-source-ai-model.aspx?s=ct_in_070225). This project shows that with a smart data strategy, you don't need a massive budget to create a capable reasoning model. The team released not only the model but also the training dataset and code, making it a truly open-source effort that can be replicated and built upon.![Image 15: Figure from the "Sky-T1: Train your own O1 preview model within $450" article](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1364,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8865a313-2326-4f07-a6dc-72cc94cb2ebe_1364x570.png)
Image 15: Figure from the "Sky-T1: Train your own O1 preview model within $450" article (Source [novasky-ai.github.io](https://novasky-ai.github.io/posts/sky-t1/))

Another approach is pure RL at a smaller scale, as shown by the **TinyZero** project. This 3B parameter model was trained for under $30 on specific tasks like countdown and multiplication, and it successfully reproduced the emergent self-verification and reasoning traces seen in the much larger DeepSeek-R1-Zero [[26]](https://github.com/Jiayi-Pan/TinyZero). This reinforces the idea that cold-start RL can produce useful behaviors even without massive compute resources, making it a viable path for experimentation and research on a tight budget [[27]](https://www.dailycal.org/news/campus/research-and-ideas/campus-researchers-replicate-disruptive-chinese-ai-for-30/article_a1cc5cd0-dee4-11ef-b8ca-171526dfb895.html).![Image 16: A figure from the TinyZero repository showing that the model is capable of self-verification.](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6111f4b4-cfb9-494c-8390-ec251702914b_1600x955.png)
Image 16: A figure from the TinyZero repository showing that the model is capable of self-verification. (Source [TinyZero GitHub Repository](https://github.com/Jiayi-Pan/TinyZero))

Finally, a new concept called **"journey learning"** offers a different perspective on training data. Traditional SFT, or "shortcut learning," trains models only on correct solution paths. In contrast, journey learning involves training on the entire problem-solving trajectory, including incorrect paths, self-corrections, and reflections [[28]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18982).![Image 17: Journey learning, as opposed to traditional shortcut learning, includes wrong solutions paths in the SFT data. Annotated figure from the O1 Replication Journey: A Strategic Progress Report – Part 1](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7a0bfcd0-6d93-4c91-a0d6-28178839b7cf_1492x724.png)
Image 17: Journey learning, as opposed to traditional shortcut learning, includes wrong solutions paths in the SFT data. (Source [O1 Replication Journey: A Strategic Progress Report – Part 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18982))

By exposing the model to mistakes and how to recover from them, journey learning helps build more robust self-correction mechanisms. A model trained only on "golden paths" can become brittle and struggle when it encounters novel errors. Training on the complete journey reinforces the model's ability to identify and correct its own mistakes, leading to more resilient and reliable reasoning. With just 327 training samples, this approach has been shown to outperform conventional supervised learning by over 8% on the MATH dataset, demonstrating its powerful potential with very little data.

Future directions will likely involve combining insights from these budget-friendly projects. Hybrid pipelines that use distillation for efficiency, small-scale RL to elicit emergent behaviors, and journey learning to build robustness could offer a balanced approach to developing capable and cost-effective reasoning models.

## Conclusion

We have explored four primary approaches for building and improving reasoning models, each with its own set of trade-offs. **Inference-time scaling** offers a way to boost performance without any training, but it comes at a high serving cost and increased latency. This method is flexible and can be applied to any model, but it is not a free lunch. **Pure RL** provides deep research insights and can unlock emergent behaviors like self-correction, though it can be unstable and difficult to control. It is a powerful tool for discovery but may not be the most practical for production.

The **SFT+RL hybrid** represents a production-ready blueprint that balances performance and reliability, making it a strong candidate for building robust, general-purpose reasoning models. It combines the best of both worlds, using SFT to provide a stable foundation and RL to explore and refine advanced capabilities. Finally, **distillation** offers an efficient method for transferring capabilities to smaller models, democratizing access to advanced reasoning, though its performance is ultimately limited by the teacher model. It is a practical solution for deploying reasoning on a budget.

The future of reasoning models will likely be a hybrid one. The most capable systems will probably combine robust SFT+RL training pipelines with sophisticated inference-time scaling techniques. This is the pattern we suspect underlies models like o1 and will likely define the next generation of o3-class systems. This combination allows models to have strong foundational reasoning abilities baked into their weights, which can then be further amplified on demand for particularly difficult problems. This adaptive computation is key to balancing performance and efficiency.

For AI engineers, the key takeaway is to match the technique to the constraints of the project. Instead of defaulting to the largest proprietary model, carefully consider your budget, performance targets, and latency requirements. For applications that need state-of-the-art reasoning on complex tasks and can tolerate higher costs, an SFT+RL model with inference-time scaling might be the best choice. For edge devices or cost-sensitive applications, a distilled model could provide the right balance of performance and efficiency. By understanding the trade-offs between these four approaches, you can make strategic decisions and build AI systems that are not only powerful but also practical and sustainable.
