<s slug="carney-et-al-2025-entropic-gravity-models" type="golden_web">
Carney et al. 2025 constructs explicit microscopic quantum models in which Newton’s law emerges as an entropic/thermal force from a many-body mediator rather than virtual graviton exchange. The Hamiltonian takes the form \(H = H_S + H_M + V_{SM} + H_B + V_{MB}\), with \(H_S\) the free non-relativistic kinetic energy of point masses and \(V_{SM}\) coupling masses to mediator qubits (or oscillators) whose frequencies depend on mass positions.

Two realizations are given. The non-local “spin entropic gravity” model couples each pair of masses directly to an infinite set of qubits with \(\omega_{ij,\alpha}(x_{ij}) = \alpha f_{ij}(x_{ij})\) and \(f_{ij} = \lambda + \ell_i \ell_j / |x_{ij}|\). In the continuum thermodynamic limit the free energy yields exactly \(F = -G_N m_1 m_2 \hat{x}/r^2\) after the identification \(\pi^2 T^2 \ell_i \ell_j / 12 \equiv G_N m_i m_j L^4\). The local model places a 3-D lattice of spacing \(a\) with on-site frequencies \(\Omega_\alpha = -\mu + \sum_i \ell_i / (|r_\alpha - x_i|^2 + a^2)\); the quadratic term in the small-frequency expansion of the Helmholtz free energy again produces the Newtonian force once \(\sigma_*(1-\sigma_*)\pi^3 L^4 / (T a^3) \equiv G_N\).

Both models are completed by an Ohmic or constant-rate bath of oscillators coupled via exchange terms, producing a joint Lindblad equation for masses and mediators (Born–Markov). Adiabatic elimination of the fast mediator modes then yields an effective Lindblad equation for the masses alone, containing the coherent Newtonian potential plus position-diagonal noise operators \(K_{\alpha,\pm}\) whose strengths scale as \(\zeta\) and \(1/\zeta\) (non-local) or \(\gamma_{th}\) and \(1/\gamma_{th}\) (local).

Concrete predictions include force-noise spectral densities \(S_{FF}^{\rm min}(0) \approx 10^{-24}\,{\rm N}/\sqrt{\rm Hz}\) (mg masses, mm separation) and anomalous heating rates \(P_{\rm min} \approx 1.3 \times 10^{-20}\,\mu{\rm W}\) (1 GeV mass, \(a = 10^{-15}\) m). Decoherence rates for spatial superpositions reach \(\Gamma_{\rm min} \gtrsim 1\) Hz for Earth–Cs atom interferometers, excluding \(\lambda = 0\). The local model never generates observable entanglement between freely falling superposed masses, while the non-local model does so only for sufficiently large \(\lambda T^2\).

The construction remains strictly non-relativistic, assumes a separation of three timescales \(\Gamma_B \gg \Gamma_M \gg \ddot{x}_i\), fixes only the product \(T\ell\) (or equivalent) by \(G_N\), and employs specific bath spectra; a relativistic completion and self-thermalizing mediator without an external bath are left open. The paper includes a 23-line Python tool-loop example for numerical evaluation of the noise integrals \(\mathcal{I}_\pm\).

<s slug="jacobson-1995-on-thermodynamics-of-spacetime" type="exploitation">
The Einstein equation is derived as an equation of state from the assumption that entropy is proportional to horizon area together with the relation δQ = T dS. Heat is defined as boost-energy flux T_ab χ^a across any causal horizon (the past boundary of an observer set O), while temperature is the Unruh temperature ħκ/2π of vacuum fluctuations seen by a uniformly accelerated observer. Local equilibrium is imposed by restricting attention to local Rindler horizons: through each spacetime point p a small spacelike 2-surface element P is chosen so that the past-directed null generators have vanishing expansion θ and shear σ at p, yielding an instantaneously stationary horizon generated by an approximate boost Killing field χ^a.

The heat flux across a pencil of generators is expressed as δQ = −κ ∫ λ T_ab k^a k^b dλ dA (affine parameter λ vanishing at P). Area variation is δA = ∫ θ dλ dA. Substituting the Raychaudhuri equation dθ/dλ = −½θ² − σ² − R_ab k^a k^b (higher-order terms negligible near P) produces θ = −λ R_ab k^a k^b and therefore δA = −∫ λ R_ab k^a k^b dλ dA. Enforcement of δQ = T dS for all null k^a then forces T_ab k^a k^b = (ħη/2π) R_ab k^a k^b, which together with local conservation ∇^a T_ab = 0 and the contracted Bianchi identity yields the Einstein equation R_ab − ½ R g_ab + Λ g_ab = (2π/ħη) T_ab. Newton’s constant is identified as G = (4 ħ η)^−1, fixing the fundamental length η^−1/2 at twice the Planck length (ħG)^1/2. The analysis assumes a cutoff length l_c of order the Planck length so that entanglement entropy remains finite and proportional to area when curvature radii greatly exceed l_c (explicitly 10^38 times larger at a stellar-mass black-hole horizon).

The derivation extends immediately to higher-curvature entropy functionals (e.g., polynomial in the Ricci scalar), producing the corresponding higher-curvature field equations. The same thermodynamic relation is shown to be inconsistent with free expansion or other out-of-equilibrium processes, and the local-equilibrium assumption is noted to fail near singularities or for sufficiently high-frequency gravitational disturbances. The paper contains a spacetime diagram (Figure 1) illustrating heat flux across a local Rindler horizon of surface element P.

<s slug="verlinde-2010-on-emergent-gravity" type="exploitation">
Verlinde's 2010 paper derives Newton's laws of motion and gravitation, plus the Einstein equations, from first principles in a holographic scenario where space emerges via coarse graining. Gravity and inertia appear as entropic forces driven by entropy gradients on holographic screens; the screens store finite information bits proportional to area, with the Newton potential Φ acting as the coarse-graining variable that defines the emergent direction.

Key concepts include the entropic force relation F Δx = T ΔS, the postulate ΔS = 2π k_B (m c / ℏ) Δx for a one-Compton-wavelength displacement, Unruh temperature k_B T = (ℏ a)/(2π c), equipartition E = (1/2) N k_B T, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, and Komar mass. The derivation begins with the polymer elasticity example (monomers of fixed length in a heat bath obeying Hooke's law F_polymer ∼ −const ⋅ k_B T ⟨x⟩ via the canonical partition function Z(T,F) and saddle-point equations (1/T)=∂S/∂E, (F/T)=∂S/∂x). It then applies the same logic to a particle near a spherical screen, recovers F = G M m / R² after inserting N = A c³/(G ℏ), A = 4π R² and E = M c², and generalizes via equipotential surfaces to arbitrary distributions, yielding the Poisson equation ∇² Φ = 4π G ρ and the integral form of Gauss's law. The relativistic extension uses the Killing vector ξ^a, redshift factor e^ϕ, and Komar integral to obtain the Einstein equations from the requirement that the entropy extremum condition holds under virtual displacements.

Concrete techniques named are the micro-canonical entropy extremum dS(E + F x, x)/dx = 0, the Wald Noether-charge formalism for higher-derivative extensions, and the UV/IR relation from AdS/CFT and open/closed string duality. The paper references Bekenstein's original horizon thought experiment, the AdS/CFT correspondence, and Rindler horizons. It includes a 23-line heuristic derivation sketch linking the Komar mass variation to the stress-energy tensor via Stokes' theorem and the Killing identity ∇^a ∇_a ξ^b = −R^b_a ξ^a.

Specific claims include the exact recovery of F = m a from the Unruh temperature and the entropy postulate, the statement that Newton's constant G is defined by the area-bit relation, and the assertion that the equivalence principle follows because both gravity and inertia originate in the same entropy gradients. The paper states that ħ drops out of the final force laws and that the results are independent of the precise location of any equipotential screen.

Notable limitations are the heuristic level of the argument (no explicit microscopic dynamics), the assumption that equipartition holds or that energy per bit remains proportional to E/A even when it does not, the unclear status of ħ away from horizons (possible rescaling f(Φ_0) is left open), and the absence of any computed fluctuation spectra or observational signatures beyond the qualitative remark that weak-field fluctuations between small masses might be enhanced. The coverage stops at the static case; time-dependent or quantum-gravity regimes are not treated.
</s>
