1 Introduction and motivation

These lecture notes is based on the pedagogical lectures given at ST4 in 2025 (https://st4physics.wixsite.com/2025). We had a total of six lectures, each 1.5 hours. These lectures aim to provide a partial overview of AdS/CFT correspondence (in general “holography”) to the graduate students and early career postdocs unfamiliar with this research area. For more detailed understanding of holography, see also [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8] apart from the references mentioned in this lecture notes. So, keeping this in mind, we will discuss some basics of conformal field theory (CFT) in sec. 2, anti de Sitter (AdS) spacetime in sec. 3 and then discuss the AdS/CFT correspondence, followed by Maldacena’s conjecture, which requires some basic knowledge of string theory and DD branes, so we discuss these also before the lecture on Maldacena’s conjecture in sec. 4. To make the readers more comfortable, we discuss three consistency checks of AdS/CFT correspondence in sec. 5: although there are many nowadays. We encourage the readers to look at more literature. The AdS/CFT correspondence has been generalized to de Sitter space and flat spacetime; hence, in the last lecture, we briefly discussed how holography works in these backgrounds. Prerequisites for these lectures are quantum field theory and general theory of relativity. We discuss the basics before every advanced topic so that readers can follow these lectures easily.

We can study the weak coupling regime of quantum field theory using perturbation theory. In the strongly coupled regime, we can’t apply perturbation theory. The question one can ask is how to explore the strongly coupled regime of QFTs? Remarkably, in 1997, Maldacena proposed a duality between N=4N=4 SYM theory in four dimensions and type I​I​BIIB supergravity in A​d​S5×S5AdS_{5}\times S^{5} background [13]. Here SYM is understood as “supersymmetric Yang-Mills”222In Electrodynamics, we have an abelian gauge field, whereas in Yang-Mills theories we have non-abelian gauge fields.. The duality proposed by Maldacena relates the strongly coupled regime of QFTs and the weakly coupled regime of gravitational theories in general. There is a mapping between the parameters of these two different theories. Therefore, we can compute the relevant quantities of QFTs in the bulk theory using AdS/CFT duality and then translate our results to QFTs using the aforementioned mapping. The AdS/CFT duality has been tested in several examples, see [14, 15, 16, 17], and it is important because

  • •

    AdS/CFT duality is needed to explore the strongly coupled regime of quantum field theories.

  • •

    AdS/CFT paradigm can resolve the information paradox [18, 19].

  • •

    It has applications in condensed matter physics (AdS/CMT) [20, 21], black holes [22, 23, 24], QCD [25, 26, 27, 28], open effective field theory [29], flat space [30, 34, 31, 32, 33], cosmological singularities [35, 36, 37], higher spin systems [38] , etc.

  • •

    One can construct multiverse models using holography [39, 40, 42, 43, 41].

We provided a few cases where AdS/CFT is useful, but there are also more. After Maldacena’s seminal work [13], we have studied this duality a lot for AdS spacetime. But we are living in a universe that is different from AdS. We are living in de Sitter space, which has a positive cosmological constant. How holography works in de Sitter space is not well understood yet. We will discuss various versions of dS holography in sec. 6.1 and then make some comments on the holography in flat space in sec. 6.3. With all these discussions in these lectures, we end our lectures and encourage the readers to go through the literature to learn more about holography.

2 Conformal field theory (CFT)

Conformal field theory is quantum field theory with an extra symmetry that includes translations, Lorentz rotations, scale transformations, and special conformal transformations. We will first discuss CFT in higher dimensions [sec 2.1] and then CFT in two dimensions in sec. 2.2. Lecture on CFT in higher dimensions is based on [44].

4 The AdS/CFT correspondence

From the earlier lectures, we see that the symmetry group of CFT in dd dimensions is the same as the symmetry group of AdS spacetimes in d+1d+1 dimensions. This hints toward the correspondence between C​F​TdCFT_{d} and A​d​Sd+1AdS_{d+1} spacetime, i.e., “Einstein gravity with negative cosmological constant in (d+1)(d+1) dimensions is dual to dd dimensional CFT living at the boundary of A​d​Sd+1AdS_{d+1} spacetime.” A cartoon picture of this statement can be seen from Fig. 1, where we have to consider A​d​Sd+1AdS_{d+1} gravity in the bulk and C​F​TdCFT_{d} at the boundary. 5.1 Holographic entanglement entropy (HEE)

Entanglement entropy in quantum mechanics (QM) : To compute the entanglement entropy for quantum mechanical systems, consider a bipartite system whose state is denoted by |ψ⟩A​B|\psi\rangle_{AB}. The density matrix of this system is defined as:

5.2 Holographic complexity

The notion of quantum circuit complexity provides a measure of how challenging it is to prepare a desired target state |ΨT⟩|\Psi_{T}\rangle from a chosen reference state |ΨR⟩|\Psi_{R}\rangle. Concretely, one may express the transformation as

|ΨT⟩=UT​R​|ΨR⟩=gn​gn−1​gn−2​…​…​…​g2​g1​|ΨR⟩,\displaystyle|\Psi_{T}\rangle=U_{TR}|\Psi_{R}\rangle=g_{n}\ g_{n-1}\ g_{n-2}.........g_{2}\ g_{1}|\Psi_{R}\rangle\,, (5.15)

where the unitary UT​RU_{TR} is built from a sequence of elementary gates gi{g_{i}}. The circuit complexity of |ΨT⟩|\Psi_{T}\rangle is then defined as the minimal number of gates required to implement such a unitary. Importantly, in quantum systems the complexity continues to increase even after thermalization, a behavior that reflects the linear growth of the black hole interior on the gravitational side [65].

In holography, the complexity of the boundary theory is calculated using quantum information theoretic tools, and several competing proposals have been put forward. One such proposal is the “complexity = volume” (CV) conjecture [66, 67, 68, 69, 70], where the complexity is associated with the volume of a codimension-one extremal hypersurface in the bulk spacetime. Other well-studied proposals include “complexity = action” [71], “complexity = spacetime volume” [72], and the more recent “complexity = anything” perspective [73, 74]. Reviews and comparisons of these approaches can be found in [75, 76, 77].

Focusing on the CV proposal, studies of eternal black holes: which are dual to thermofield double states, together with the ER=EPR conjecture [78], indicate that the spatial volume of the Einstein-Rosen bridge grows linearly with boundary time. This growth is the analogue of the linear increase in circuit complexity of the dual quantum state [66, 67, 68, 69, 70]. In this case, the complexity is estimated as

C​(t)∼Vol​(Σt)GN​R,C(t)\sim{{\rm Vol}(\Sigma_{t})\over G_{N}R}\,, (5.16)

where Σt\Sigma_{t} is an extremal codimension-one spacelike slice anchored at boundary time tt, RR is the AdS curvature radius, and GNG_{N} the Newton constant.

In static spacetimes, the dominant contribution to Vol​(Σt){\rm Vol}(\Sigma_{t}) arises from the near boundary region. Introducing a cutoff ϵ\epsilon in a geometry with did_{i} spatial boundary directions yields the scaling relation for the complexity as

so that the global symmetry group of a boundary conformal field theory (BCFT) is S​O​(2,d−1)SO(2,d-1). This residual symmetry acts as the conformal group on the (d−1)(d-1)-dimensional boundary, while bulk operators transform in representations of this reduced conformal algebra.

To construct the gravity dual of a dd dimensional BCFT, we extend the dd dimensional manifold MdM_{d} into a (d+1)(d+1) dimensional bulk spacetime Nd+1N_{d+1} such that the boundary of the bulk geometry satisfies

∂Nd+1=M∪Q,\partial N_{d+1}=M\cup Q, (6.16)

where MM corresponds to the asymptotic AdS boundary and QQ is the EOW brane. See Fig. 14 for the pictorial illustration.

We consider the gravitational action

I=116​π​GN​∫Ndd+1​x​−g​(R−2​Λ)+18​π​GN​∫Qdd​x​−h​(K−T),\displaystyle I=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\int_{N}d^{d+1}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left(R-2\Lambda\right)+\frac{1}{8\pi G_{N}}\int_{Q}d^{d}x\sqrt{-h}\,\left(K-T\right), (6.17)

where RR, Λ\Lambda, and GNG_{N} denote the Ricci scalar, cosmological constant, and Newton’s constant, respectively. Further, hh, KK, and TT are the induced metric, trace of the extrinsic curvature, and the tension of the EOW brane.

Varying the action (6.17) with respect to the induced metric on the EOW brane yields

δ​I=116​π​GN​∫Qdd​x​−h​(Ka​b−(K−T)​ha​b)​δ​ha​b.\displaystyle\delta I=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\int_{Q}d^{d}x\sqrt{-h}\left(K_{ab}-(K-T)\,h_{ab}\right)\delta h^{ab}. (6.18)

If we impose Dirichlet boundary conditions on QQ, then δ​ha​b=0\delta h^{ab}=0. For Neumann boundary conditions (NBC), we obtain

Ka​b−(K−T)​ha​b=0.\displaystyle K_{ab}-(K-T)\,h_{ab}=0. (6.19)

Taking the trace of this equation gives the tension of the EOW brane as follows

ha​b​Ka​b−(K−T)​ha​b​ha​b=0\displaystyle h^{ab}K_{ab}-(K-T)\,h^{ab}h_{ab}=0
⟹K−(K−T)​d=0\displaystyle\implies K-(K-T)\,d=0
⟹T=(d−1d)​K.\displaystyle\implies T=\left(\frac{d-1}{d}\right)K. (6.20)

The holographic dual of BCFT is obtained as follows. Since the bulk theory is Einstein gravity with a negative cosmological constant, we localize gravity on the EOW brane, a setup known as braneworld holography. The gravity living on the EOW brane is further dualized using the AdS/CFT correspondence. Since holography is applied twice, this construction is known as double holography. We consider the bulk metric in the following form

d​s2=d​ρ2+cosh2⁡(ρL)​d​sA​d​Sd2,\displaystyle ds^{2}=d\rho^{2}+\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{L}\right)ds^{2}_{AdS_{d}}, (6.21)

where ρ∈(−∞,∞)\rho\in(-\infty,\infty) and constant ρ\rho slices correspond to dd dimensional AdS spacetime. The dd dimensional AdS metric is given by

d​sA​d​Sd2=L2y2​[−d​t2+d​y2+d​ω→2],\displaystyle ds^{2}_{AdS_{d}}=\frac{L^{2}}{y^{2}}\left[-dt^{2}+dy^{2}+d\vec{\omega}^{2}\right], (6.22)

with ω→∈ℝd−2\vec{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-2}. In (d+1)(d+1) dimensions, the cosmological constant is

Λ=−d​(d−1)2​L2.\displaystyle\Lambda=-\frac{d(d-1)}{2L^{2}}. (6.23)

Introducing new coordinates zz and xx as below

z=ycosh⁡(ρL),x=y​tanh⁡(ρL).\displaystyle z=\frac{y}{\cosh\left(\frac{\rho}{L}\right)},\qquad x=y\tanh\left(\frac{\rho}{L}\right)\,. (6.24)

Using the above, the metric (6.21) becomes

d​s2=L2z2​[d​z2−d​t2+d​x2+d​ω→2].\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{L^{2}}{z^{2}}\left[dz^{2}-dt^{2}+dx^{2}+d\vec{\omega}^{2}\right]. (6.25)

To construct the gravity dual of BCFT, we place the EOW brane QQ at ρ=ρ∗\rho=\rho_{*} and restrict the range of ρ\rho to −∞<ρ<ρ∗-\infty<\rho<\rho_{*}. For the metric (6.21), the extrinsic curvature is

Ka​b=1L​tanh⁡(ρL).\displaystyle K_{ab}=\frac{1}{L}\tanh\left(\frac{\rho}{L}\right). (6.26)

Using this expression together with the Neumann boundary condition (6.2), we obtain the tension of the EOW brane as

T=(d−1L)​tanh⁡(ρ∗L).\displaystyle T=\left(\frac{d-1}{L}\right)\tanh\left(\frac{\rho_{*}}{L}\right). (6.27)

Wedge holography101010For AdS spacetime, the construction of wedge holography was first developed in [104, 105]. This framework was subsequently extended to flat spacetime in [106], where holographic dualities were explored in the absence of a cosmological constant. More recently, wedge holography has been generalized to de Sitter (dS) spacetime in [41], providing a codimension two holographic description of spacetime with positive cosmological constant. provides an example of a co dimension two holographic correspondence. Realization of wedge holography can be understood in a two-steps: (i) one considers the localization of (d+1)(d+1)-dimensional Einstein gravity onto dd-dimensional Karch-Randall branes embedded in the bulk. This mechanism, known as braneworld holography [107, 108], yields an effective gravitational theory on each brane, (ii) since the induced geometry on the Karch-Randall branes is asymptotically AdS, the gravitational dynamics on the branes admit a dual description in terms of a conformal field theory (CFT) living on their (d−1)(d-1)-dimensional boundaries, in accordance with the AdS/CFT correspondence [13].

Combining these two steps, one obtains wedge holography. The wedge holographic dictionary is as follows: “(d+1)(d+1)-dimensional Einstein gravity in the bulk region bounded by the Karch-Randall branes (wedge region) is dual to a (d−1)(d-1)-dimensional defect CFT living at the intersection (corner) of the branes”. 6.3 Flat space holography from wedge holography perspective

In this section, we aim to discuss holography for flat spacetime (Λ=0\Lambda=0). There are various progresses in this direction, see for example [30, 34, 31, 32, 33]. In this lecture, we will focus on just one case, which is wedge holography in flat spacetime, and it is based on the paper [106]111111In this section, figures are also taken from [106]..

6.3.1 Hyperbolic and de Sitter slicing of flat spacetime

Let us begin with a flat spacetime of dimension d+1d+1, namely ℝ1,d\mathbb{R}^{1,d}:

d​s2=−d​T2+d​R2+R2​d​Ωd−12.ds^{2}=-dT^{2}+dR^{2}+R^{2}d\Omega_{d-1}^{2}. (6.28)

The spacetime admits a decomposition into hyperbolic slices HdH^{d} and de Sitter slices d​SddS^{d}, a structure that points toward holography [109].

The hyperbolic slicing is obtained by the coordinate transformation

T=η​cosh⁡ρ,R=η​sinh⁡ρ,\displaystyle T=\eta\cosh\rho,\ \ R=\eta\sinh\rho\,, (6.29)

which brings the metric (6.28) into the form

d​s2=−d​η2+η2​(d​ρ2+sinh2⁡ρ​d​Ωd−12),[hyperbolic patch],\displaystyle ds^{2}=-d\eta^{2}+\eta^{2}(d\rho^{2}+\sinh^{2}\rho d\Omega_{d-1}^{2}),\ \ \ \mbox{[hyperbolic patch]}, (6.30)

Alternatively, the de Sitter slicing arises from the parametrization

T=r​sinh⁡t,R=r​cosh⁡t,\displaystyle T=r\sinh t,\ \ \ R=r\cosh t, (6.31)

leading to the metric (6.28) as

ds2=dr2+r2(−dt2+cosh2tdΩd−12).[de Sitter patch],\displaystyle ds^{2}=dr^{2}+r^{2}(-dt^{2}+\cosh^{2}td\Omega_{d-1}^{2}).\ \ \ \ \mbox{[de Sitter patch]}, (6.32)

In both coordinate systems the radial variables (η\eta and rr) range as 0≤η<∞0\leq\eta<\infty and 0≤r<∞0\leq r<\infty. By gluing the two patches smoothly at η=0\eta=0 and r=0r=0, one recovers the complete four-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, as illustrated in the left panel of Fig. 16.

We impose a regularization on the coordinates η\eta and rr as

0≤η≤η∞, 0≤r≤r∞.\displaystyle 0\leq\eta\leq\eta_{\infty},\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\leq r\leq r_{\infty}. (6.33)

so that the hyperbolic and de Sitter patches are effectively compactified to HdH^{d} and d​SddS_{d}, in direct analogy with wedge holography in AdS [104][105], which itself is viewed as a doubled extension of AdS/BCFT [102, 103]. Extending this wedge holographic construction to (d+1d+1)-dimensional Minkowski spacetimes suggests a possible “duality between a (d−1)(d-1)-dimensional CFT living on Sd−1S^{d-1} and gravity in the wedge region (6.33)”. As in the AdS/CFT correspondence [13] and dS/CFT duality [89], it is natural to introduce a UV cutoff in the boundary CFT, corresponding to the geometric cutoff

ρ≤ρ∞,t≤t∞.\displaystyle\rho\leq\rho_{\infty},\ \ \ t\leq t_{\infty}. (6.34)

In what follows, we analyze the hyperbolic and de Sitter slices independently via the application of wedge holography.

6.3.2 Wedge holography for hyperbolic slices

The authors began by formulating wedge holography for the hyperbolic slicing, illustrated in the left panel of Fig. 17. The (d+1)(d+1)-dimensional wedge WhW^{h} is defined by restricting the coordinate η\eta in the hyperbolic patch (6.30) to the range

η1≤η≤η2.\displaystyle\eta_{1}\leq\eta\leq\eta_{2}. (6.35)

At the two boundaries η=η1\eta=\eta_{1} and η=η2\eta=\eta_{2}, we place end-of-the-world (EOW) branes, denoted by Qh​(1)Q^{h(1)} and Qh​(2)Q^{h(2)}, on which we impose Neumann boundary conditions

Ka​b−ha​b​K=−T​ha​b,\displaystyle K_{ab}-h_{ab}K=-Th_{ab}, (6.36)

where Ka​bK_{ab} is the extrinsic curvature (with outward-pointing normal nan^{a}) and TT denotes the brane tension. The Neumann boundary conditions (NBC) are satisfied provided the tension of the branes are

Th​(i)=d−1d​Kh​(i)=d−1ηi,\displaystyle T^{h(i)}=\frac{d-1}{d}K^{h(i)}=\frac{d-1}{\eta_{i}}, (6.37)

with i=1,2i=1,2 being labels of two EOW branes. By analogy with wedge holography in AdS [104], authors proposed that “gravity in the (d+1)(d+1)-dimensional wedge region WhW^{h} (6.35) is dual to a (d−1)(d-1)-dimensional CFT living on the sphere Sd−1S^{d-1} at the asymptotic tip ρ→∞\rho\to\infty”. A cutoff ρ=ρ∞\rho=\rho_{\infty} as in (6.34) regulates the boundary theory. The authors provided evidence for this duality via the explicit computations of the partition function, holographic entanglement entropy, and scalar perturbations.

Each hyperbolic slice HdH^{d} at fixed η\eta preserves an S​O​(1,d)SO(1,d) symmetry, corresponding to the Lorentz group of the parent (d+1)(d+1)-dimensional Minkowski spacetime. This matches the conformal symmetry of the Euclidean CFT on Sd−1S^{d-1}. In the special case d=3d=3, the symmetry enhances to two copies of the Virasoro algebra, originating from the superrotation symmetry in ℝ1,3\mathbb{R}^{1,3} which is naturally identified with the conformal symmetry of the dual two-dimensional CFT.

The analysis further done by the authors indicates that the dual CFT on Sd−1S^{d-1} is non-unitary. This feature is expected: the interval in the time-like direction (6.35), orthogonal to the hyperbolic slices HdH^{d} has been included as an internal dimension, even though each slice separately admits a standard AdS/CFT interpretation. The situation is closely analogous to dS/CFT, where the dual CFT is likewise non-unitary, as inferred from central charge arguments [90] and supported by explicit realizations [113, 110, 111, 112, 83].

6.3.3 Wedge holography for de Sitter slices

As a second example of flat space wedge holography, consider the (d+1)(d+1)-dimensional wedge Wd​sW^{ds}, defined by restricting the de Sitter slicing (6.32) to the region

r1≤r≤r2,,\displaystyle r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2},, (6.38)

as illustrated in the right panel of Fig. 17. The two boundaries at r=r1r=r_{1} and r=r2r=r_{2} are taken to be EOW branes, denoted Qd​s​(1)Q^{ds(1)} and Qd​s​(2)Q^{ds(2)}, where we impose the Neumann boundary condition (6.36). Solving this condition yields the brane tensions

Td​s​(i)=d−1d​Kd​s​(i)=d−1ri,\displaystyle T^{ds(i)}=\frac{d-1}{d}K^{ds(i)}=\frac{d-1}{r_{i}}, (6.39)

where i=1,2i=1,2 denotes two end-of-the-world branes.

The authors proposed that “gravity on the wedge Wd​sW^{ds}(6.38) is holographically dual to a (d−1)(d-1)-dimensional CFT defined on a sphere Sd−1S^{d-1}. Although the wedge has two asymptotic boundaries located at t=−∞t=-\infty and t=∞t=\infty, they are identified through the antipodal map. A cutoff t=±t∞t=\pm t_{\infty}, analogous to (6.34), regulates the theory. As in the hyperbolic case, each de Sitter slice d​SddS_{d} at a fixed value of radial coordinate exhibits an S​O​(1,d)SO(1,d) symmetry, corresponding to the Lorentz symmetry of the ambient (d+1)(d+1)-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, which in turn matches the conformal symmetry of the Euclidean CFT on Sd−1S^{d-1}. In the case d=3d=3, this symmetry is enhanced to two copies of the Virasoro algebra, arising from the superrotation symmetry of ℝ1,3\mathbb{R}^{1,3} [114, 115], and is naturally identified with the conformal symmetry of a dual two-dimensional CFT.

This setup can be viewed as a de Sitter analogue of wedge holography in AdS [104], with the wedge defined by extending a d​SddS_{d} slice along a spatial width. Consequently, the dual CFT on Sd−1S^{d-1} is again expected to be non-unitary, in close analogy with the dS/CFT correspondence [89, 90, 113, 110, 111, 112, 83]. Authors provided support to this duality via computing the partition function, holographic entanglement entropy, and scalar field perturbation.
